    National Dog Day falls on August 26 and is one of the few "national days" with both a documented founder and a documented reason for its date. Colleen Paige, a pet lifestyle expert and animal welfare advocate, founded it in 2004 and chose August 26 because it was the day her family adopted her first dog from a shelter, when she was ten. The day's stated purpose is narrower than general dog appreciation: it is an adoption campaign.

    History

    Most "national days" cannot tell you who started them. This one can. National Dog Day was founded in 2004 by Colleen Paige, a pet and family lifestyle expert and animal rescue advocate who also founded National Puppy Day, National Mutt Day and National Cat Day. Her stated mission is to help galvanize the public to recognize the number of dogs needing rescue each year, and to acknowledge family dogs alongside dogs that work to save lives and bring comfort.

    The date is not arbitrary, which is rarer still. Paige's site explains it: August 26 is the day her family adopted her first dog, a Sheltie, from the local animal shelter, when she was ten. UPI and NBC New York report the same explanation, but both are repeating the founder's account rather than confirming it, and no source gives the year.

    The day's official standing is softer than usually stated. UPI reported in 2022 that it "was officially recognized by the New York State Senate in a 2013 resolution," and Paige's site says the same. This site could not retrieve the resolution from the New York State Senate's own database to confirm it, and the UPI article contradicts itself: its photo caption gives 2004 for the recognition and the wrong date, August 24, for the holiday. Treat it as reported rather than verified. One position on the founder's site is worth noting, because observances of this kind rarely take one: National Dog Day is against any kind of breed ban.

    The problem the day points at is documented better elsewhere. Per ASPCA figures compiled by Shelter Animals Count, 2.8 million dogs entered U.S. shelters and rescues in 2025; about 2 million were adopted and 320,000 were euthanized. Dog euthanasia fell nearly 7 percent against 2024, and the overall rate has dropped from 10 percent in 2019 to 8 percent in 2025. One trend runs the other way: the ASPCA notes that the time dogs, especially large dogs, spend in shelters before adoption has increased over the past five years.

    Timeline

    • 2004 — Colleen Paige founds National Dog Day, choosing August 26 because it is the date her family adopted her first dog, a Sheltie, from a local shelter when she was ten.
    • 2005 — Paige founds National Cat Day, one of several animal observances she creates alongside National Puppy Day and National Mutt Day.
    • 2013 — Per the founder's site and a later UPI report, New York State recognizes the day. This site could not retrieve the resolution from the state Senate's legislative database to verify it.
    • 2019 — The shelter euthanasia rate for dogs and cats stands at 10 percent, per ASPCA figures compiled by Shelter Animals Count.
    • 2022 — UPI reports on the day's founding, repeating Paige's account of why the date is August 26.
    • 2025 — 2.8 million dogs enter U.S. shelters and rescues. About 2 million are adopted and 320,000 euthanized, with dog euthanasia down nearly 7 percent against 2024 and the overall rate down to 8 percent.

    How to observe

    The founder's suggestions are specific, and most cost nothing. Consider adoption first. If you want a particular breed, look for a breed-specific rescue rather than a breeder; they exist for most breeds and they are full. If you do go to a breeder, check licensing, reviews and local references, including from a veterinarian. Paige's site warns against pet stores supplied by puppy mills, backyard breeders, internet listings and newspaper ads. Her lowest-effort suggestion is a five-dollar donation to a local shelter on August 26, for people who do not have a dog and are not getting one.

    If you want to move a number rather than a sentiment, ask your shelter about its longest-staying dog instead of its newest arrival. Length of stay for large dogs is the part of the problem the ASPCA's own data says is getting worse, and those are the dogs that adoption-day traffic walks past.

    Frequently asked questions

    Is National Dog Day a federal holiday?

    No. It has no federal status, no day off and no federal proclamation. The founder's site reports recognition by New York State in 2013, which this site could not verify directly.

    Who started National Dog Day?

    Colleen Paige, a pet lifestyle expert and animal welfare advocate, in 2004. She also founded National Cat Day, National Puppy Day and National Mutt Day. This is unusual: most observances of this kind have no documented founder at all.

    Why is it on August 26?

    Because that is the date Paige's family adopted her first dog, a Sheltie, from a local animal shelter when she was ten. The explanation comes from Paige's own site, and the news coverage repeating it is repeating her.

    Does National Dog Day take a position on anything?

    Yes, one. The founder's site states that National Dog Day is against any kind of breed ban, a more specific stance than most days of this kind take.

    How many dogs are actually in U.S. shelters?

    Per ASPCA figures compiled by Shelter Animals Count, 2.8 million dogs entered U.S. shelters and rescues in 2025. About 2 million were adopted, 542,000 returned to their owners and 320,000 euthanized.
