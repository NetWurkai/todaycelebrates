    Geographic Information Systems Day, usually just GIS Day, is the mapping world's open house: one day on which county GIS departments, universities and utilities show the public what they do with spatial data. Esri, the software company that runs the day's website, dates the first formal GIS Day to 1999 and credits the consumer advocate Ralph Nader with inspiring it. It falls on the third Wednesday of November, inside Geography Awareness Week, which puts it on November 18 in 2026.

    History

    GIS Day's origin is better attributed than most observances of its kind, and the attribution comes from the company at its center. "Esri president and cofounder Jack Dangermond credits activist Ralph Nader as the person who inspired the first formal GIS Day in 1999," reads the overview page at gisday.com, which Esri maintains. Esri's own ArcNews said the same in 2014, adding Nader's condition: "He wanted GIS Day to be a grassroots effort and open to everyone to participate." Esri does not claim to have founded the day.

    The date comes from somewhere else. ArcNews gives the rule plainly: "The event traditionally is held the third Wednesday of November each year during Geography Awareness Week, a geographic literacy initiative sponsored by the National Geographic Society." That week has a federal paper trail. Proclamation 5700, signed September 8, 1987, records that "The Congress, by Public Law 100-78, has designated the week of November 15 through November 21, 1987," and proclaims that week as Geography Awareness Week. Neither the proclamation nor its text in the Statutes at Large makes the observance recurring - it names 1987 and no other year - but the week continued, and the American Association of Geographers, which hosts it, describes it as "Held each year during the third week of November" and "Established in 1987 by presidential proclamation." The association lists November 16-20 for 2026, a week whose Wednesday is November 18.

    Two caveats belong on the record. First, National Geographic's education blog gave a different account in 2008, dating the week to a joint resolution signed on January 6, 1987 and referring to a "1986 proclamation"; neither matches Proclamation 5700 or Public Law 100-78, and this page follows the proclamation and the statute as the more primary sources rather than splitting the difference. Second, Esri had not posted a 2026 date when this page was written - its overview still showed November 19, 2025 - so the date here rests on the stated rule and the 2026 Geography Awareness Week dates, not on a published announcement.

    Timeline

    • September 8, 1987 - Proclamation 5700 proclaims November 15-21, 1987 as Geography Awareness Week, noting that Congress designated the week in Public Law 100-78.
    • 1999 - The first formal GIS Day is held; Jack Dangermond credits Ralph Nader with inspiring it.
    • 2013 - One of the largest GIS Days to that point: more than 1,000 hosts and about 105,000 attendees in 98 countries.
    • November 19, 2014 - Esri's ArcNews tells readers to mark the calendar for GIS Day 2014, the third Wednesday of that November.
    • November 19, 2025 - The most recent date published on Esri's gisday.com, again the third Wednesday.
    • November 16-20, 2026 - Geography Awareness Week for 2026, per the American Association of Geographers; GIS Day falls on its Wednesday, November 18.

    How to observe

    Find an event rather than a hashtag. Esri's gisday.com carries event listings and the planning material for hosting one, and the day was built to be hosted locally: Esri describes it as "dedicated to sharing, teaching, and inspiring others with GIS."

    If your county or city has a GIS department, look for its public map portal. Parcel boundaries, zoning, flood zones and aerial imagery are often published there and free to browse.

    Treat it as the Wednesday of a longer week. The American Association of Geographers runs Geography Awareness Week around it - November 16-20 in 2026, under the theme "Mapping the Future."

    Frequently asked questions

    When is GIS Day?

    On the third Wednesday of November, during Geography Awareness Week. Esri states that rule, and in 2026 that Wednesday is November 18. Because the day moves, a fixed November 18 is right for 2026 and wrong for most other years.

    Who created GIS Day?

    No single founder is claimed. Esri, which runs gisday.com, says its cofounder Jack Dangermond credits Ralph Nader with inspiring the first formal GIS Day in 1999, and that Nader wanted it to be grassroots and open to anyone. Esri does not say it founded the day.

    How is GIS Day related to Geography Awareness Week?

    The week is the container. Congress designated November 15 through 21, 1987 as Geography Awareness Week in Public Law 100-78, and Proclamation 5700 of September 8, 1987 proclaimed it; the week has been kept up each year since in the third week of November. GIS Day is the Wednesday inside it.

    How many people take part?

    The only figures found in an acceptable source are Esri's for 2013: more than 1,000 hosts and about 105,000 attendees across 98 countries. Esri called it one of the biggest to that point, and published no comparable count afterward that could be located.

    Is GIS Day a federal holiday?

    No. No federal designation of a GIS Day was found, and federal offices, banks and schools operate normally. The 1987 proclamation behind Geography Awareness Week named only that one week in 1987.
