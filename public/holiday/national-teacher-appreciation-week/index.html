    National Teacher Appreciation Week runs Monday through Friday of the first full week of May, with National Teacher Appreciation Day on the Tuesday. The National Education Association established the week in 1985, in the same decision that moved the single day out of March. The date on this page is the Monday the week opens.

    History

    The week is younger than the day it contains. A National Teacher Day existed, intermittently, from 1953, when Eleanor Roosevelt persuaded the 81st Congress to proclaim one after years of letters from an Arkansas teacher named Mattye Whyte Woodridge. Congress returned to the subject once more, declaring March 7, 1980 National Teacher Day — and only that one date, in that one year.

    What the National Education Association did in 1985 was turn an on-and-off single day into a fixed annual week. The NEA Representative Assembly voted that year to move the observance to the Tuesday of the first full week of May, and Teacher Appreciation Week was established around that Tuesday. The NEA describes the week as officially established by the NEA in 1985, and it has run on the same rule since.

    That rule is worth stating plainly, because it is the part people get wrong: the week is not the first week of May, it is the first full week — the first Monday-to-Friday span that falls entirely inside the month.

    Timeline

    • c. 1944 — Arkansas teacher Mattye Whyte Woodridge begins writing to political and education leaders about a national day honoring teachers.
    • 1953 — Eleanor Roosevelt, one of her correspondents, persuades the 81st Congress to proclaim a National Teacher Day.
    • 1980 — Congress declares March 7 National Teacher Day, for that year only, after lobbying by the NEA and its Kansas and Indiana affiliates.
    • 1985 — The NEA Representative Assembly moves the observance to the Tuesday of the first full week of May; Teacher Appreciation Week is established around it.
    • 2026 — The week runs May 4–8, with National Teacher Appreciation Day on Tuesday, May 5.

    How to observe

    Start by working out the dates, since they shift by up to six days year to year. In 2026 the week ran May 4–8; in 2027 it runs May 3–7.

    Spread the recognition across the five days rather than stacking it on Tuesday. The NEA itself distinguishes observances inside the week, listing National Black Teachers Day separately from National Teacher Appreciation Day, which is a reminder that the week has room for more than one gesture.

    If you are organizing on behalf of an institution rather than a family, the NEA frames the week as an opportunity for school boards, administrators, community members, and students to put appreciation on the record. A proclamation read into the minutes of a May board meeting outlasts a catered lunch.

    And check whether your district already has something scheduled before you plan a parallel event. Districts and states frequently run their own versions, and two uncoordinated celebrations in one week ask more of teachers than they give.

    Frequently asked questions

    When is National Teacher Appreciation Week?

    It is the first full week of May — the first Monday-to-Friday span that falls entirely within the month. In 2026 that was May 4–8; in 2027 it is May 3–7.

    Is it the first week of May or the first full week?

    The first full week, which is not always the same thing. If May opens mid-week, the first full week begins on the following Monday, which is why the dates can move by nearly a week from one year to the next.

    How is the Week different from National Teacher Appreciation Day?

    The Day is the Tuesday inside the Week. The NEA treats that Tuesday as the focal point, with the surrounding Monday-to-Friday span as the broader observance.

    Who established National Teacher Appreciation Week?

    The National Education Association, in 1985, in the same decision by its Representative Assembly that moved National Teacher Day from March to the first full week of May.

    Is it a federal holiday?

    No. Neither the week nor the day inside it has federal status, and no school or workplace closes for it. The dates are set by the NEA rather than by statute.
