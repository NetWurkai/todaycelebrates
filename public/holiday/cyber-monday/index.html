    Cyber Monday is the Monday after Thanksgiving, and unlike most days on this calendar it has a documented birth date and a traceable motive. It was invented in November 2005 by Shop.org, the online-retail arm of the National Retail Federation, to give internet stores their own answer to Black Friday. Shop.org's own executive director conceded at the time that it was not actually the biggest online shopping day of the year.

    History

    Cyber Monday began as a name in search of a day. Shop.org, the online-retail division of the National Retail Federation, was looking for a way to promote internet shopping, settled on the Monday after Thanksgiving, and announced it in a press release dated November 21, 2005 that described Cyber Monday as "one of the biggest online shopping days of the year."

    The observation underneath the name was real enough. Shop.org's 2005 eHoliday survey, conducted with BizRate Research, found that 77 percent of online retailers said their sales had risen substantially on the Monday after Thanksgiving the previous year. The explanation offered at the time was the office connection: more than a third of the 1,890 consumers surveyed said they would use internet access at work to shop. In 2005, with home broadband far from universal, a workplace connection was often the fastest one a shopper had.

    What the name was not, by the account of the people who chose it, was a description of a record. Scott Silverman, Shop.org's executive director, told BusinessWeek that the term came out of a brainstorm, and that alternatives including Black Monday, Blue Monday and Green Monday were discarded along the way. "It's not the biggest day," he said. "But it was an opportunity to create some consumer excitement." The growth around it was not invented, though: comScore Networks expected American online sales for that November and December to rise 24 percent, to more than $19 billion.

    That is an unusually honest founding document for a shopping holiday, and it is worth keeping in mind every November, when the day is described as a record that was discovered rather than a label that was applied.

    Timeline

    • 2004 — Online retailers see a substantial sales increase on the Monday after Thanksgiving; 77 percent report as much in the Shop.org/BizRate Research survey conducted the following year.
    • November 21, 2005 — Shop.org issues the press release that coins "Cyber Monday," describing it as one of the biggest online shopping days of the year.
    • November 28, 2005 — The first Cyber Monday, the Monday after that year's Thanksgiving.
    • November 2005 — Scott Silverman, Shop.org's executive director, tells BusinessWeek the name came from a brainstorm and that "It's not the biggest day."
    • November-December 2005 — comScore Networks expects US online holiday sales to rise 24 percent, to more than $19 billion.

    How to observe

    Treat the date as the arbitrary part and the prices as the thing to check. Because Cyber Monday was scheduled rather than discovered, there is no particular reason a given retailer's best online price of the year should land on it; many run the same promotion across the whole weekend, and some run it for a week.

    If you want to read the day's own paperwork, both pieces are short. The Shop.org survey finding that prompted the name is quoted in CNN/Money's 2005 report, and Silverman's "It's not the biggest day" remark is in CBS News's coverage from the same week. Reading them is a good inoculation against the annual claim that the day is historically unprecedented.

    It is also worth noticing what has quietly expired. The original rationale for the day was that work had faster internet than home. That stopped being true some time ago, and the day has outlived its own reasoning.

    Frequently asked questions

    Is Cyber Monday a federal holiday?

    No. The 11 legal public holidays listed in 5 U.S.C. 6103(a) do not include it, and it has no official standing of any kind. It is a promotional date created by a retail trade group.

    Who invented Cyber Monday?

    Shop.org, the online-retail division of the National Retail Federation, in November 2005. Contemporaneous reporting credits the organization rather than one person: Shop.org's executive director described the name as the product of a group brainstorm. Accounts naming a single individual as the coiner circulate widely, but no primary source consulted for this page supports one.

    When is Cyber Monday?

    The Monday after Thanksgiving. Since federal law sets Thanksgiving as the fourth Thursday in November, Cyber Monday falls between November 26 and December 2.

    Was Cyber Monday really the biggest online shopping day when it was named?

    No, and the people who named it said so at the time. Scott Silverman of Shop.org told BusinessWeek, "It's not the biggest day. But it was an opportunity to create some consumer excitement." The press release's description of it as "one of the biggest online shopping days of the year" rested on a survey in which retailers reported a Monday sales bump the year before.

    Why was it tied to Monday rather than the weekend?

    The original reasoning was practical. Shoppers returning to work after Thanksgiving had access to faster internet connections there than most had at home, and more than a third of consumers in the 2005 Shop.org survey said they would shop using internet access at work.
