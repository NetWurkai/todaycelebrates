    Africa Industrialization Day falls on November 20 by proclamation of the UN General Assembly, which designated it in December 1989 within the framework of the Second Industrial Development Decade for Africa. It is an advocacy day rather than a celebration: its subject is manufacturing on the continent and what stands in the way of more of it. Since 2018 the date has anchored a longer African Union event, Africa Industrialisation Week.

    History

    The day was announced in Africa before the UN acted on it. The UN's own observance page places the announcement at the twenty-fifth Ordinary Session of the Assembly of the Organisation of African Unity, held in Addis Ababa in July 1989: "Africa Industrialization Day was announced on November 20 during this session." The General Assembly then proclaimed the date in December 1989, by resolution A/RES/44/237, "within the framework of the Second Industrial Development Decade for Africa (1991-2000)." The UN page gives the month and year of the resolution but not its day, and does not say which year the day was first observed — only that the UN system "has held events on that day throughout the world" since, and that "Since 2018, Africa Industrialization Day has been commemorated with weeklong events."

    What the day argues for has moved with the UN's industrial decades for Africa. The second ran to 2000. By 2026 the Secretary-General's message for the day was pointing instead to "the start of the Fourth Industrial Development Decade for Africa," alongside "The African Continental Free Trade Area, bold actions towards Agenda 2063" — Agenda 2063 having been adopted in January 2015 by the African Union's heads of state and government. The message names "electric vehicles, to batteries, computer chips and renewable energy" as the sectors gaining momentum, and the Africa Credit Rating Agency and the "Sevilla Commitment, agreed in 2025" among the instruments behind them. It contains no statistic.

    Who runs the day is less clear than who proclaimed it. The UN's page never names an organizer, and mentions UNIDO not as the day's host but as the agency "tasked with leading the implementation of the Decade." The week around the date belongs to the African Union, which calls Africa Industrialisation Week "a flagship initiative of the African Union, established in 2018." AIW 2026 ran in Lusaka, Zambia, from November 17 to 20, 2026, under the theme "Financing Industrialization for Inclusive and Sustainable Development," convened through the AU Commission's department for economic development, trade, industry and mining and aimed at "ministries of finance, central banks, DFIs, private investors, and global partners." The AU's event page puts "Africa's industrial financing gap" at "USD 280-400 billion annually" — its own estimate, for which the page cites no source.

    Timeline

    • July 1989 — At the twenty-fifth Ordinary Session of the OAU Assembly in Addis Ababa, Africa Industrialization Day is announced on November 20.
    • December 1989 — The UN General Assembly proclaims 20 November Africa Industrialization Day, by resolution A/RES/44/237.
    • 1991-2000 — The Second Industrial Development Decade for Africa, the framework the proclamation was made within.
    • January 2015 — The African Union's heads of state and government adopt Agenda 2063.
    • 2018 — The day begins to be marked with weeklong events; the African Union establishes Africa Industrialisation Week as a flagship initiative.
    • 17-20 November 2026 — AIW 2026 is held in Lusaka, Zambia, under the theme "Financing Industrialization for Inclusive and Sustainable Development."

    How to observe

    Read the Secretary-General's message. It is about a page, published on the UN's observance page for the day, and it is the shortest statement of where the UN currently thinks African industrialization stands — including what it leaves out.

    Follow the week rather than the day. Africa Industrialisation Week is where the substance is, and the 2026 edition was built around money rather than policy: finance ministries, central banks and development finance institutions instead of industry ministries. The African Union publishes the program and the outcomes.

    Go to statistical sources for figures, not to observance pages. Neither the UN's page for the day nor the Secretary-General's 2026 message contains a single number about African manufacturing — a useful thing to notice before quoting an awareness day on the subject it exists to raise awareness about.

    Frequently asked questions

    Is Africa Industrialization Day an official UN observance?

    Yes. The General Assembly proclaimed 20 November as Africa Industrialization Day in December 1989, by resolution A/RES/44/237, and it appears on the UN's list of international days.

    Why November 20?

    The UN's page says the day was announced on November 20 during the OAU Assembly's twenty-fifth Ordinary Session in July 1989, but no source found for this page explains why that date was chosen.

    Who organizes it?

    Not clearly stated. The UN says only that the UN system holds events worldwide on the day, and names UNIDO as the agency leading the industrial decade rather than the day. The surrounding Africa Industrialisation Week is run by the African Union.

    Is it marked in the United States?

    It is an international observance rather than a national holiday anywhere, including the United States. Nothing closes; the UN system holds events in various countries, and the main event is held in Africa.

    What was the theme for 2026?

    Africa Industrialisation Week 2026, in Lusaka, used "Financing Industrialization for Inclusive and Sustainable Development." The UN's observance page publishes no separate theme for the day itself.
