    Nature

    The autumnal equinox is a moment rather than a day: the instant the center of the Sun crosses the plane of Earth's equator heading south, which begins astronomical autumn in the Northern Hemisphere and spring south of the equator. The U.S. Naval Observatory puts the 2026 equinox at September 23 at 00:05 Universal Time, which is the evening of September 22 across the United States. Its name promises equal day and night, and that is the one thing it does not quite deliver.

    History

    The autumnal equinox has no founder and no founding date, because it is not an invented observance. It is an astronomical event: the moment the center of the Sun crosses the plane of Earth's equator. Seasons exist because Earth is tilted 23.5 degrees on its axis, and the equinoxes are the two moments each year when that axis is tilted neither toward the Sun nor away from it. In September the Sun crosses from the northern side of the equatorial plane to the southern, and keeps traveling south until the December solstice. For an observer on the equator the Sun passes directly overhead at noon, and the North and South Poles are lit at the same time, which is true at no other point in the year.

    The name is a near miss. "Equinox" comes from the Latin aequus, equal, and nox, night, and day and night are not in fact equal. The Sun is a disk rather than a point of light, and the atmosphere bends its rays so that it appears above the horizon while its true position is still below. Both effects add daylight. On and around the equinox, NOAA's National Weather Service gives the length of day as roughly 12 hours and 6.5 minutes at the equator, 12 hours and 8 minutes at 30 degrees of latitude, and 12 hours and 16 minutes at 60 degrees. NASA is blunter: the split is not precisely equal, and the date on which it comes closest depends on your latitude.

    How to observe

    Because the equinox is an instant, the first thing to do is find out when it falls where you are. The Naval Observatory's Astronomical Applications Department publishes the exact Universal Time of every equinox and solstice; apply your own offset from UTC and you have the moment. For 2026 that is 00:05 UT on September 23, which is late evening on September 22 in the United States, and it is why American sources date the equinox to the 22nd while the table says the 23rd. The 2027 equinox is September 23 at 06:02 UT, landing on the 23rd in Eastern time and the 22nd in Pacific.

    The better project takes a week. Look up sunrise and sunset for your own location across the days around the equinox and find the date on which they come closest to twelve hours apart. It will not be the equinox, and how far off it is depends on how far you live from the equator.

    Frequently asked questions

    When is the autumnal equinox in 2026?

    September 23 at 00:05 Universal Time, according to the U.S. Naval Observatory. In American time zones that is the evening of September 22 — 5:05 p.m. Pacific Daylight Time, by NASA's reckoning — which is why most U.S. listings give September 22.

    Are day and night exactly equal on the equinox?

    No. NASA states that the length of day and night on the equinox is not precisely equal. The Sun is a disk rather than a point, and atmospheric refraction lifts its image above the horizon before it has truly risen, so daylight runs a little over twelve hours: about 12 hours and 6.5 minutes at the equator and about 12 hours and 16 minutes at 60 degrees of latitude. The National Weather Service's own glossary says daylight is "everywhere 12 hours" on the equinox, which is the convenient version rather than the measured one.

    What does "equinox" mean?

    It is Latin: aequus, meaning equal, and nox, meaning night.

    Is the autumnal equinox the first day of fall?

    It is the start of astronomical autumn in the Northern Hemisphere. The same moment begins spring in the Southern Hemisphere, where the days are getting longer rather than shorter.

    Is the equinox always September 22?

    No. The Naval Observatory gives September 23 at 00:05 UT for 2026 and September 23 at 06:02 UT for 2027, while the National Weather Service glossary describes the date as approximately September 22. Both are true at once: the moment is fixed; the calendar date depends on your time zone.
