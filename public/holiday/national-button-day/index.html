    National Button Day falls on November 16 and is almost always credited to the National Button Society, founded in 1938. The Society's own pages make no such claim: they use the day's name without saying who created it or when. The 1938 attached to the day is the organization's founding year, borrowed.

    History

    The origin of this observance is not well documented. The version repeated most often is that National Button Day was founded in 1938 by the National Button Society, and the American Sewing Guild puts it in exactly those terms — "National Button Day was founded in 1938 by the National Button Society and recognizes button collecting as an organized hobby" — but cites nothing for it. Nothing on the Society's public site claims the day at all. The only place it appears is a members' notice headed "Celebrate National Button Day, November 16th," which announces a slide presentation and gives no origin. So the organization usually named as founder marks the day without claiming to have created it, and the year attached to the day belongs to the organization rather than to the observance.

    The Society itself is well documented. Its history page states: "The National Button Society (NBS) was founded in 1938, which led to the creation of many state and local button clubs. The first NBS button show was held the next year, in Chicago." It arrived in the middle of a boom — "Button collecting became a craze in the United States in the 1930s. When NBS formed in 1938, we set a standard for classifying and collecting buttons that resonates to this day." It now reports over 2,000 members spanning four continents, with 39 of the 50 states represented by state and local button clubs, publishes the National Button Society Bulletin five times a year, and holds a convention each year, usually in August.

    Buttons were ornaments before they were fasteners. Cooper Hewitt, Smithsonian Design Museum, writing about its own collection, describes them as "initially used to adorn men's wear as a symbol of wealth and status," calls the eighteenth century "the great period of button design," and traces the word to the French bouton, a bud or a knob. On the earliest part the Society hedges, and correctly: "Many believe that French guilds in the 1300s created the first buttons to be jewelry worn on garments as aristocratic status symbols." Many believe, not the record shows.

    Timeline

    • 1300s — French guilds are often credited with the first buttons, worn as jewelry and status symbols rather than as fasteners. The National Button Society's own history reports this as what "many believe," not as established fact.
    • 18th century — Cooper Hewitt calls this "the great period of button design."
    • 1930s — Button collecting becomes a craze in the United States.
    • 1938 — The National Button Society is founded and sets a standard for classifying and collecting buttons.
    • 1939 — The first NBS button show is held, in Chicago.
    • 1982 — Cooper Hewitt mounts its "Button, Button" exhibition and publishes Carl Christian Dauterman's catalog of its button collection.

    How to observe

    Look at real ones. Cooper Hewitt has digitized its button holdings, collected largely by Sarah and Eleanor Hewitt on their travels through Europe and given or bequeathed to the museum, and publishes them as a searchable set of images.

    Read the catalog. "Buttons in the Collection of the Cooper-Hewitt Museum," by Carl Christian Dauterman, was published by the museum in 1982 and can be read free through Smithsonian Libraries and the Internet Archive.

    Then do the unglamorous part, which is the actual hobby: sort the jar. The Society's standing claim is that it set the standard for classifying and collecting buttons in 1938, and a club is how people learn it — 39 states have one, standard membership starts at $45 a year, and the Bulletin arrives five times a year.

    Frequently asked questions

    Who started National Button Day?

    Not established. The National Button Society is usually credited, and the American Sewing Guild states that the day was founded in 1938 by the Society, but it gives no source and the Society's own pages do not claim the day.

    Why is the day dated to 1938?

    Because that is the year the National Button Society was founded. No source connects 1938 to the observance itself rather than to the organization.

    Is National Button Day about sewing buttons or campaign badges?

    Sewing buttons. The day attaches to button collecting as a hobby and to the National Button Society, whose subject is the fastening-and-ornament kind.

    Is the National Button Society still active?

    Yes. It reports over 2,000 members across four continents, clubs in 39 of the 50 states, a bulletin five times a year and an annual convention, usually held in August.

    Where can I see historic buttons online?

    Cooper Hewitt, Smithsonian Design Museum, has digitized its button collection and publishes it as a searchable image set, and the museum's 1982 button catalog is free to read through Smithsonian Libraries.
