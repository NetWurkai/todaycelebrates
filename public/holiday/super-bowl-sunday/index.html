    Super Bowl Sunday

    Super Bowl Sunday is the day the National Football League plays its championship game. Nobody declared it a holiday — the league simply schedules the game as part of each season, and the country rearranged its Sunday around it. Encyclopædia Britannica describes the day as having become an unofficial American holiday, which is the closest thing to a formal status it has.

    History

    The game exists because of a merger. The NFL and the rival American Football League agreed to combine in 1966, and the agreement called for an end-of-season championship game between the two leagues' champions. The merger itself was not finalized until 1970, so the first game was played while the leagues were still separate: January 15, 1967, at the Los Angeles Memorial Coliseum, where the NFL's Green Bay Packers beat the AFL's Kansas City Chiefs 35–10. It was carried on two television networks and played before less than a sellout crowd — the only one of these games that has not sold out.

    It was also not called the Super Bowl. That first game was the AFL-NFL World Championship Game. The name "Super Bowl" first appeared in 1969, and the Roman numerals arrived with it. Because each game is played in a calendar year after the season it decides, the numerals are what keep the game and its season from being confused.

    What the name attached to is now a commercial event as much as a football game. Every Super Bowl after the first has sold out, and advertising time during the broadcast is the most expensive of the year: a 30-second spot cost roughly $4.8 million in 2016. Since the 1980s, public and press attention to the commercials has nearly matched attention to the game itself — which is an honest thing to say about a day whose observance, for a large share of the audience, consists of watching advertisements.

    Timeline

    • 1966 — The NFL and the American Football League agree to merge; the agreement calls for an end-of-season championship game between the two leagues' champions.
    • January 15, 1967 — The first game, the AFL-NFL World Championship Game, is played at the Los Angeles Memorial Coliseum. Green Bay beats Kansas City 35–10, on two television networks and before less than a sellout crowd.
    • 1969 — The name "Super Bowl" appears for the first time, along with the Roman numerals used to designate each game.
    • 1970 — The NFL-AFL merger is finalized; from the 1970–71 season the game decides the NFL championship between the AFC and NFC champions.
    • 2025–26 season — Super Bowl LX: the Seattle Seahawks beat the New England Patriots 29–13.
    • February 14, 2027 — Super Bowl LXI is scheduled for SoFi Stadium in Los Angeles, televised by ESPN, with the NFC champion as the designated home team.

    How to observe

    Look the date up rather than assuming it. The NFL fixes the game inside each season's postseason schedule, so it moves. Super Bowl LXI is set for Sunday, February 14, 2027, at SoFi Stadium in Los Angeles, with ESPN carrying the broadcast. That date is Valentine's Day, which is worth noticing well before the second week of February if you owe someone dinner.

    One detail that changes how the game looks on screen: for Super Bowl LXI the NFC champion is the designated home team and gets the choice of uniform color. The "home" team at a Super Bowl plays in a neutral stadium, so the designation is purely about jerseys and locker rooms.

    If the football history interests you more than the broadcast, the Pro Football Hall of Fame in Canton, Ohio has been maintained by the NFL since 1963 and is where the primary record of the game's early years is kept — including the years when the championship had a duller name and could not fill a stadium.

    Frequently asked questions

    Is Super Bowl Sunday always on the same date?

    No. The NFL schedules the game as part of each season's postseason, so the date moves; Britannica describes the game as played in January or February. Super Bowl LXI is set for Sunday, February 14, 2027.

    Is Super Bowl Sunday a holiday?

    Not an official one. It has no federal or state status and is not among the federal holidays, so nothing closes for it. Britannica calls it an unofficial American holiday, which describes the behavior rather than any designation.

    Why was the first Super Bowl not called the Super Bowl?

    Because the name did not exist yet. The January 15, 1967 game was the AFL-NFL World Championship Game, and "Super Bowl" first appeared in 1969.

    Why do Super Bowls use Roman numerals?

    Each game is played in a calendar year after the season it settles, so a plain year would be ambiguous. The numerals came in with the name in 1969 and identify the game itself.

    Where is the 2027 Super Bowl being played?

    SoFi Stadium in Los Angeles, on Sunday, February 14, 2027 — Valentine's Day. ESPN has the broadcast, and the NFC champion will be the designated home team.
