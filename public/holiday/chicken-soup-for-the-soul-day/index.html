    Chicken Soup for the Soul Day marks a book that almost nobody wanted to publish. The first volume appeared in 1993, after — by its co-author's own count — 144 publishers had turned the manuscript down, and the series went on to become one of the largest franchises in American trade publishing. The observance itself has no documented origin; the book's history, unusually for a day like this one, does.

    History

    In 1992 Peter Vegso received a manuscript from two unpublished motivational speakers, Jack Canfield and Mark Victor Hansen. Vegso and his partner Gary Seidler ran Health Communications Inc., a small publishing house they had started in South Florida with a printing press bought on arrival. HCI was not the first publisher to see the manuscript: it had been shopped to large New York houses — Random House, Simon & Schuster, Harcourt, McGraw Hill — with the help of literary agent Jeff Herman, and by Canfield's count 144 publishers rejected it before HCI accepted it. Forbes reported that HCI's break-even point on the book was 10,000 to 15,000 copies.

    The book was published in 1993, and it was not released in major bookstore chains. Vegso did not believe in advertising. He offered the two editors a 20 percent cut of sales, split between them, but they had to sell it themselves: they left copies at dentists' offices and in beauty parlors, "anywhere that merchants would allow them to." By 2003 Forbes reported that the original book had sold nearly 8 million copies and the series nearly 70 million across more than 60 titles, and that about 70 percent of HCI's roughly $52 million in annual sales came from the franchise.

    The brand's later history belongs to a different company. Chicken Soup for the Soul Entertainment, a publisher of books that had expanded into television, film and ad-supported streaming — Redbox, Crackle, Popcornflix — bought the DVD-kiosk business Redbox in 2022. In March 2024 it disclosed in a regulatory filing that it might not be able to continue as a going concern, and in late June 2024 it filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection in Delaware, reporting about $414 million in assets against $970 million in debts owed to more than 500 creditors.

    Timeline

    • 1992 — Peter Vegso, who with Gary Seidler had started the South Florida publisher Health Communications Inc., receives a manuscript from two unpublished motivational speakers, Jack Canfield and Mark Victor Hansen.
    • 1993 — The first Chicken Soup for the Soul is published, after what Canfield counted as 144 rejections. It is not released in major bookstore chains, and the two editors sell it themselves.
    • 2003 — Forbes reports more than 60 titles in the series, nearly 70 million copies sold across it, and nearly 8 million for the original book alone.
    • August 2022 — Chicken Soup for the Soul Entertainment finalizes its acquisition of Redbox, whose kiosk network then numbers about 36,000.
    • March 2024 — The company discloses in a regulatory filing that it may be unable to continue as a going concern.
    • Late June 2024 — It files for Chapter 11 in Delaware bankruptcy court, reporting about $414 million in assets against $970 million in debts owed to more than 500 creditors.

    How to observe

    The honest way to mark the day is to read one, which costs almost nothing. The series sold in such quantities that used copies turn up everywhere — library sales, thrift stores, the free shelf at a laundromat. Pick an edition from the 1990s rather than a recent one; the early volumes are a readable record of what mainstream American sentiment sounded like before the internet reshaped it.

    If the business interests you more than the stories, notice what the books were not. They were not in chain bookstores, not advertised, and not reviewed. Two authors sold them by hand in waiting rooms, on a royalty they split, for a publisher whose break-even was fifteen thousand copies. That is the actual lesson of the franchise, and a more durable one than any single story inside it.

    A third option takes about a minute: give one away. The books were built to be handed to someone having a bad week, and they still work for that.

    Frequently asked questions

    Who created Chicken Soup for the Soul Day?

    No source that meets this site's standard records who established the observance, or when. The November 12 date circulates widely on daily-holiday calendars, none of which cite an origin. Read it as a date the internet settled on rather than one anybody declared.

    Did 144 publishers really reject the book?

    That is the figure Forbes reported in 2003, attributed to Jack Canfield's own count. It is his account rather than a publisher's record, so it is worth reading as a claim by an interested party — but it is at least a sourced claim, which is more than most retellings of the story offer.

    Who published the first book?

    Health Communications Inc., a small South Florida house run by Peter Vegso and, until the late 1990s, his partner Gary Seidler. The large New York publishers had already passed on it.

    Is Chicken Soup for the Soul still in business?

    Chicken Soup for the Soul Entertainment — the company that held the brand along with Redbox, Crackle and Popcornflix — filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection in Delaware in late June 2024, owing close to $1 billion. Whether and how the book publishing business continues separately is not something these sources settle.

    Is it a federal holiday?

    No. It has no official status of any kind, and no government body recognizes it.
