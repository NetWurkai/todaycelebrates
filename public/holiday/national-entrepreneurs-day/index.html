    National Entrepreneurs' Day falls on the third Tuesday of November, which in 2026 is November 17. Three resolutions in Congress proposed that rule and none of them enacted it; the only federal document that has ever named a specific day for it is a presidential proclamation from 2024. The month around it has firmer standing than the day does.

    History

    The day's paper trail starts in the House. On October 30, 2013, Representative Scott H. Peters of California introduced H.Res. 401, titled "Expressing support for designation of the third Tuesday of November as National Entrepreneurs Day," with 26 cosponsors; it went to the Committee on Energy and Commerce. The wording matters: the resolution supports a designation rather than making one, resolving only that the House "supports the designation of National Entrepreneurs Day." The published bill record shows no action after the referral. Peters' own office, announcing the effort on November 19, 2013, said the resolution's inspiration "came in response to the passing of San Diego innovator Duane Roth earlier this year."

    The Senate followed twice. Senator Jerry Moran of Kansas, with Senator Cory Booker of New Jersey, submitted S.Res. 582 on November 18, 2014 — the same construction, with no apostrophe in the title — and it went to the Committee on Commerce, Science, and Transportation with no further action recorded. They submitted it again as S.Res. 314 on November 17, 2015, and that one went further: on November 19, 2015 the committee was discharged and the resolution was considered and agreed to. One chamber, in one session, expressing support. The day has never been designated in law.

    Who created it is contested, and both accounts come from congressional offices. Moran's releases in 2014 and 2015 say the day "was established in 2010 by David Hauser and Siamak Taghaddos, co-founders of Grasshopper," together with Amir Tehrani of The Legacy Foundation, and that the group petitioned President Obama. Peters' release describes launching the day in 2013 through his own resolution and mentions neither 2010 nor those names. Neither office is the more primary source for the other's claim, and the proclamation record does not settle it: Proclamation 8747 of November 1, 2011 proclaims "November 2011 as National Entrepreneurship Month" and never uses the phrase National Entrepreneurs' Day.

    The month is where the federal recognition sits. Proclamation 10850 of October 31, 2024 proclaims November 2024 as National Entrepreneurship Month and calls on Americans "to celebrate November 19, 2024, as National Entrepreneurs' Day" — the third Tuesday of that month, and the same rule the resolutions had proposed.

    Timeline

    • November 1, 2011 — Proclamation 8747 proclaims November 2011 as National Entrepreneurship Month. It names no single day.
    • October 30, 2013 — Rep. Scott Peters introduces H.Res. 401 with 26 cosponsors, supporting designation of the third Tuesday of November; it is referred to the Committee on Energy and Commerce.
    • November 18, 2014 — Sens. Jerry Moran and Cory Booker submit S.Res. 582, referred to the Committee on Commerce, Science, and Transportation.
    • November 19, 2015 — S.Res. 314, submitted two days earlier, is discharged from committee and agreed to by the Senate. No designation is enacted.
    • October 31, 2024 — Proclamation 10850 proclaims November 2024 as National Entrepreneurship Month and calls for celebrating November 19, 2024 — that year's third Tuesday — as National Entrepreneurs' Day.

    How to observe

    Read S.Res. 314. It is a short course in how advocacy numbers get written. It asserts that "more than 400,000 new businesses were created in the United States in 2013" and that entrepreneurs "accounted for the creation of nearly 2,300,000 jobs in 2013," and attributes neither figure to anyone; another clause opens "research shows" without naming the research.

    Then read the 2014 version beside it, which claimed "more than 500,000 new businesses are created in the United States every month" and "roughly 3,000,000 jobs every year." A year later the same sponsors filed different figures on a different basis, and neither text cites a source. For numbers that do carry one, start with the Census Bureau's Business Formation Statistics.

    Check the date your calendar uses. If it prints November 17 every year, it is following a date rather than the rule, and it will be wrong in every year the third Tuesday lands elsewhere.

    Frequently asked questions

    When is National Entrepreneurs' Day?

    The third Tuesday of November — the rule named in all three congressional resolutions and matched by the date in the 2024 proclamation. That is November 17 in 2026, November 16 in 2027 and November 21 in 2028.

    Is National Entrepreneurs' Day a federal holiday?

    No. Nothing closes and no law designates it. The furthest it has gone in Congress is a Senate resolution expressing support for a designation, agreed to on November 19, 2015.

    Did Congress ever actually designate the day?

    No. All three resolutions were titled "expressing support for designation," and their operative text supports a designation rather than making one. Only S.Res. 314 was agreed to, and only in the Senate.

    Who started National Entrepreneurs' Day?

    Two congressional offices give different answers. Senator Moran's releases credit a 2010 effort by David Hauser and Siamak Taghaddos of Grasshopper with Amir Tehrani of The Legacy Foundation; Representative Peters' office describes launching the day in 2013 with H.Res. 401. Nothing reconciles the two.

    What is the difference between this and National Entrepreneurship Month?

    The month has been proclaimed by presidents, including November 2011 and November 2024. The day is a single date within it, proposed by the resolutions and named in the 2024 proclamation.
