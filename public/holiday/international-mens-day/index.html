    International Men's Day falls on November 19 and is marked mostly by men's health organizations, fatherhood charities and, in some years, by national parliaments. It was founded in 1999 by Dr Jerome Teelucksingh, a history lecturer at the University of the West Indies in Trinidad and Tobago. It carries no official status: it is not a United Nations observance, and the day's own coordinating body still lists formal recognition on the UN calendar among its goals for 2026.

    History

    The November 19 observance dates to 1999. The day's coordinating body states that "International Men's Day on 19 November was founded in 1999 by Dr Jerome Teelucksingh, a history lecturer at the University of the West Indies," and that he inaugurated it in Trinidad and Tobago.

    Why that date, no acceptable source says. The explanation repeated across daily-holiday sites — that Teelucksingh chose his father's birthday, or the anniversary of a 1989 Trinidad and Tobago football match — appears in no founding account, government record or news report this page could verify, so it is not stated here as fact.

    Earlier attempts used a different date entirely. By the same history, organizations in the United States, Europe and Australia held small events in February in the early 1990s at the invitation of Professor Thomas Oaster, who directed the Missouri Center for Men's Studies at the University of Missouri, Kansas City. Oaster "successfully promoted the event in 1994," it says, "but his following attempt in 1995 was poorly attended." The February idea was older still: the same page points to a 1969 New York Times report of men wanting February 23.

    After 1999 the November date spread without much paperwork, and the coordinating body says so. It records the day being promoted in India from 2007 by Uma Challa, "not knowing where the date had actually come from," and the Maltese Association for Men's Rights voting in 2009 to move its observance to November 19 at Australian organizers' request. The site itself is sponsored by Dads4Kids, an Australian fatherhood charity.

    Official recognition is thin rather than absent. The House of Commons Library records a Commons debate on International Men's Day scheduled for Thursday 21 November 2024, arranged by the Backbench Business Committee and led by Sam Rushworth MP — on the 21st rather than the 19th, which is how a day with no official date tends to be marked.

    The day's stated objectives are men's and boys' health, improving gender relations, promoting gender equality and highlighting positive male role models. The theme announced for 2026 is "The Future is Everyone."

    How to observe

    The part of this day with hard numbers behind it is men's health, and the numbers are federal. In 2023, US life expectancy at birth was 75.8 years for males and 81.1 for females — a gap of 5.3 years, 0.1 narrower than in 2022. In 2024 the age-adjusted suicide rate was 22.3 per 100,000 for males against 5.6 for females; of 48,824 suicide deaths, 38,977 were male. The National Center for Health Statistics notes the male rate "was about 3 to 4 times higher than the rate for females across the 2014 to 2024 period."

    Both come from short NCHS data briefs that take about twenty minutes to read, which is the difference between repeating a statistic and knowing which year it belongs to.

    To see what an institution does with the day rather than what the internet does with it, Commons proceedings are published in full in Hansard, where any debate held on the day can be read in the members' own words.

    The day also has a deliberate neighbor: the coordinating body pairs November 19 with the children's day observed on November 20, calling the two "48 hours of celebration."

    Frequently asked questions

    Who founded International Men's Day, and when?

    Dr Jerome Teelucksingh, a history lecturer at the University of the West Indies in Trinidad and Tobago, founded the November 19 observance in 1999. The February events organized in the early 1990s by Professor Thomas Oaster at the University of Missouri, Kansas City were a separate, earlier attempt on a different date.

    Why is it on November 19?

    No acceptable source says. The coordinating body's history gives the founder and the year but no reason for the date, and the explanations circulating on daily-holiday sites — a father's birthday, a 1989 football match — could not be traced to the founder, a government record or a news report.

    Is International Men's Day an official or United Nations day?

    No. It has no official designation and is not on the UN calendar: the coordinating body lists "Formal recognition of International Men's Day on the UN calendar" among its three goals for 2026.

    What is the theme for 2026?

    "The Future is Everyone."

    Why do health statistics come up so often on this day?

    Because men's health is one of the day's four stated objectives and the gaps are measurable: US life expectancy at birth in 2023 was 75.8 years for males against 81.1 for females, and the 2024 age-adjusted suicide rate was 22.3 per 100,000 for men against 5.6 for women.
