    Christmas Eve

    Celebration

    Christmas Eve falls on December 24, and nearly everything that makes it feel like a holiday happens after dark. It has no founder and no founding date — it is simply the evening before a fixed feast — which is why its customs vary so sharply by country, denomination and household, and why the best-documented Christmas Eves on record involve a guitar, a ceasefire and a spacecraft.

    History

    Christmas Eve is not an observance anyone established. It is the evening before one, and that accounts for most of its shape.

    In the Roman Catholic tradition the evening carries a liturgy of its own. The Canadian Conference of Catholic Bishops describes four distinct celebrations of the Eucharist at Christmas: a Vigil Mass on the evening of December 24, celebrated either before or after evening prayer; the Mass during the Night, which most people call midnight Mass, and which the bishops trace to an earlier nighttime Eucharist in Rome; a Mass at Dawn; and a Mass During the Day, which early Roman sources place at around 9 a.m., "the usual morning hour." Each has its own texts and its own appointed time, and the document is firm that they are to be celebrated at those times.

    Beyond that structure the record thins. There is no founding proclamation for Christmas Eve as a popular holiday and no first observance to date, and no single origin for the gift-giving, the late service or the particular meal — those accumulated locally, and this page does not assign them a beginning it cannot source. What the record does hold is a handful of unusually well-documented individual Christmas Eves, which is a different and more honest kind of history.

    Timeline

    • 1818 — December 24: "Stille Nacht, heilige Nacht" is first performed at the Church of St. Nikola in Oberndorf, with Mohr's words and Gruber's melody, for two solo voices, chorus and guitar.
    • 1914 — December 24 and 25: the Christmas truce on the Western Front, which most accounts have beginning with carol singing from the trenches on Christmas Eve.
    • 1955 — a child dials the unlisted number of the Continental Air Defense Command in Colorado Springs believing she is calling Santa Claus; the commander on duty, Colonel Harry Shoup, assures her CONAD will guarantee Santa a safe journey from the North Pole.
    • 1958 — the Santa-tracking tradition passes to NORAD when the command is formed.
    • 1968 — December 24: the Apollo 8 crew reads from the book of Genesis in orbit around the Moon.

    How to observe

    Sing "Silent Night" the way it was first performed, which is not the way you have heard it. Joseph Mohr, an assistant priest at the new parish of St. Nikola in Oberndorf, near Salzburg, handed his poem to Franz Xaver Gruber on December 24, 1818; by Gruber's own account the melody came back that same evening, scored for two solo voices, chorus and guitar rather than organ. It premiered in the church that night.

    Listen to the Apollo 8 broadcast. On December 24, 1968, in lunar orbit, Frank Borman, Jim Lovell and Bill Anders took turns reading from the book of Genesis — the first ten verses, as Lovell remembered it — to what Borman had been told would be the largest audience that had ever listened to a human voice, and ended with a wish for everyone "on the good Earth." NASA keeps the recording.

    Follow the Santa tracker at noradsanta.org. NORAD has run it every December 24 since the tradition passed to the command on its formation in 1958, with more than a thousand military and civilian volunteers answering the phones.

    Frequently asked questions

    Is Christmas Eve a federal holiday in the United States?

    Not in itself. December 24 is not among the eleven holidays set by 5 U.S.C. 6103. But when Christmas Day falls on a Saturday, most federal employees are given the preceding Friday instead, so on the Office of Personnel Management's 2027 calendar Friday, December 24 appears as the observed date for Christmas Day. Christmas Eve is never the holiday itself; it occasionally holds Christmas Day's observance.

    What is midnight Mass?

    One of four distinct Christmas Masses in the Roman Catholic tradition. The bishops' own term for it is the Mass during the Night, and it falls between the Vigil Mass on the evening of December 24 and the Mass at Dawn. The Canadian Conference of Catholic Bishops traces it to an earlier nighttime Eucharist in Rome.

    Did the NORAD Santa tracker start with a misprinted Sears advertisement?

    NORAD's own account does not say so. Its fact sheet describes a child in 1955 accidentally dialing the unlisted number of the Continental Air Defense Command after seeing a promotion in a local newspaper, and names neither Sears nor a misprint. The Sears advertisement and the typo come from recollections by Colonel Shoup's children, reported by NPR. The misprint is the version that circulates; it is not the version the command publishes.

    Was there a football match during the 1914 Christmas truce?

    Probably not an organized one. Accounts mention informal kick-abouts with makeshift balls, but TIME reports that organized matches were unlikely in 1914, and the widely shared British-German match was played in 2014 as a centenary commemoration.

    Where was "Silent Night" first sung?

    At the Church of St. Nikola in Oberndorf, near Salzburg, on December 24, 1818. The Silent Night Museum, which holds Gruber's own 1854 account of the occasion, states that the familiar story of a mouse gnawing through the organ bellows is not true, although contemporary documents do say the organ's sound could not fill the church.
