    National American Teddy Bear Day is marked on November 14, the date in 1902 when Theodore Roosevelt went bear hunting in Mississippi and refused to shoot the animal his hosts had cornered for him. The observance's own origin is undocumented; the hunt behind it is not. What followed was a newspaper cartoon, a Brooklyn candy shop, and the only American toy named after a president.

    History

    The National Park Service places Roosevelt on a bear hunting trip near Onward, Mississippi, on November 14, 1902, at the invitation of Governor Andrew H. Longino. The Theodore Roosevelt Center places the hunt in the vicinity of Smedes, Mississippi; the two disagree on the town. On the bear, the Center is deliberately noncommittal: it was either tied to a tree, or wounded, or a cub, or quite elderly, and all versions agree that the bear was unable to defend itself. Roosevelt refused to shoot it.

    Clifford Berryman drew the refusal for the Washington Post, where it appeared on November 16, 1902. The Library of Congress catalogs the cartoon as Drawing the line in Mississippi, a detail from a series called The Passing Show, and notes pointedly that Berryman did not invent the toy.

    Morris Michtom did, or at least an American version of it. By the Park Service's account, Michtom, a Brooklyn candy shop owner who made stuffed animals with his wife Rose, saw the cartoon, made a bear and called it Teddy's Bear; after receiving Roosevelt's permission to use the name, he mass-produced it and founded what became the Ideal Toy Company in 1903. Germany reached the same object independently: Margarete Steiff and her nephew Richard began making a plush bear in 1902, and three thousand were shipped to and sold in the United States in 1903.

    Timeline

    • November 14, 1902 — Roosevelt hunts near Onward, Mississippi, at Governor Andrew H. Longino's invitation, and refuses to shoot the cornered bear.
    • November 16, 1902 — Clifford Berryman's Drawing the line in Mississippi appears in the Washington Post.
    • 1902 — In Germany, Margarete Steiff and her nephew Richard begin making a plush bear of their own.
    • 1903 — The Michtoms begin factory production in Brooklyn and found the Ideal Toy Company; three thousand Steiff bears are shipped to and sold in the United States.
    • 1906 — The name settles into teddy bear.
    • 1963 — Benjamin Michtom presents an original Michtom bear to Kermit Roosevelt; it reaches the National Museum of American History about a year later.

    How to observe

    Look at the cartoon rather than a description of it. The Library of Congress record for Drawing the line in Mississippi is online, carries no known restrictions on publication, and is filed under reproduction number LC-USZ62-34274. The Library notes that it does not own the original drawing, and that the Archives of American Art holds a reproduction of it.

    Notice that the American story is only half of it. Steiff's bear was developed in Germany in the same year, and neither of the sources here establishes which bear came first — the parallel invention is the more interesting fact, and it is usually left out.

    And read the backlash, which was real. In 1907 the Rev. Michael G. Esper of Michigan warned his congregation about the fad for supplanting the good old dolls; a Reno newspaper answered days later with the headline Teddy Bears Rule Supreme. An original Michtom bear is now at the National Museum of American History: Benjamin Michtom presented it to Kermit Roosevelt in 1963 for the bear's sixtieth birthday, and it reached the museum about a year later.

    Frequently asked questions

    Why is it on November 14?

    November 14, 1902 is the date of Roosevelt's Mississippi bear hunt. No record of who created this observance, or why they chose that date, could be found, so the match is a coincidence of dates rather than a documented reason.

    Did Theodore Roosevelt invent the teddy bear?

    No. He refused to shoot a bear; Clifford Berryman drew it; Morris Michtom made and sold the toy and asked Roosevelt's permission to use his name. The Library of Congress record for the cartoon notes specifically that Berryman did not invent the toy either.

    Was the bear really tied to a tree?

    The accounts differ. The Theodore Roosevelt Center says the bear was either tied to a tree, or wounded, or a cub, or quite elderly, and that all versions agree it was unable to defend itself. The Center also says the origins of the teddy bear are not entirely clear.

    Did the teddy bear start in America or Germany?

    Both, in the same year, apparently without contact. Michtom's bear followed the November 1902 cartoon in Brooklyn; Margarete Steiff and her nephew Richard began a plush bear in Germany in 1902, and three thousand reached American buyers in 1903. The sources here do not settle which was first.

    Is November 14 a federal holiday?

    No. Nothing closes, and the day has no official status.
