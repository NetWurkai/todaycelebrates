    World Vegan Day falls on November 1 and is, for once, a modern observance whose paperwork is in order. The Vegan Society says the first one was held on November 1, 1994, created by Louise Wallis, to mark the Society's fiftieth anniversary. November was chosen because November 1944 is when the Society — and the word vegan itself — came into existence.

    History

    The Vegan Society dates its founding to November 1944, when Donald Watson called a meeting with five other non-dairy vegetarians, among them Elsie Shrigley, to discuss non-dairy vegetarian diets and lifestyles. The Society describes those six as the first to actively found a new movement, and says they did it despite opposition — the opposition in question coming largely from the vegetarian establishment of the day, which did not see the need for a separate word.

    The word was Watson's. By the Society's account he coined vegan from a suggestion by two early members, George A. Henderson and his wife Fay K. Henderson, that the organization be called Allvega and its magazine Allvegan. What the Society did not have was a definition of what the word meant: it was 1949, five years in, before Leslie J Cross pointed out that the society lacked one, and winter 1988 before the definition it still uses was in circulation. Forty-four years to define its own founding term is an unusually candid thing for an organization to publish about itself.

    The institutional machinery arrived in between. The society was first registered as a charity in August 1964, and became a limited company in December 1979, with its assets transferred to a new charity at the same time. The Vegan Trademark — the sunflower mark that now appears on packaging — was founded in 1990.

    The anniversary came last. Louise Wallis, whom the Society calls its former president, created World Vegan Day in 1994 to mark the fiftieth anniversary, and the first one was held on November 1, 1994. It was later extended to World Vegan Week and then to World Vegan Month, which is the form the Society runs today across the whole of November.

    One detail the Society's own pages do not settle: they call Wallis "our former President" without saying which office she held in 1994. Accounts elsewhere describe her as the Society's chair at the time. That is not what the source says, so this page does not say it either.

    Timeline

    • November 1944 — Donald Watson calls a meeting with five other non-dairy vegetarians, including Elsie Shrigley; the Vegan Society is founded and Watson coins the word vegan, from the Hendersons' suggestion of Allvega and Allvegan.
    • 1949 — Leslie J Cross points out that the society still lacks a definition of veganism.
    • August 1964 — The society is registered as a charity for the first time.
    • Winter 1988 — The definition of veganism the Society still uses is in use.
    • 1990 — The Vegan Trademark is founded, to let consumers identify products without reading labels.
    • November 1, 1994 — Louise Wallis creates World Vegan Day and the first one is held, marking the Society's fiftieth anniversary; it is later extended to World Vegan Week and then World Vegan Month.

    How to observe

    Treat it as a month, not a day. The Society extended the single day first to a week and then to World Vegan Month, and posts each year's activities on its own get-involved pages rather than leaving the date to look after itself.

    Look for the sunflower instead of reading the ingredient list. The Vegan Trademark has existed since 1990 and is the Society's own certification mark, which makes it faster and more reliable than parsing a label — particularly for non-food products, where the question is manufacturing rather than ingredients.

    Read the Society's definition of veganism before arguing about the word. It took the organization from 1944 to winter 1988 to settle on the wording, which is a useful corrective to the confidence with which the term gets thrown around in both directions.

    For the first-person version, the Society's archive holds a transcript of a 2002 interview with Donald Watson and his 2005 obituary, both linked from its history page. Watson lived from 1910 to 2005, long enough to be asked directly where the word came from.

    Frequently asked questions

    When is World Vegan Day?

    November 1, every year. The Vegan Society also runs World Vegan Month across the whole of November, which grew out of the original single day.

    Who started World Vegan Day?

    Louise Wallis, described by the Vegan Society as its former president, created it in 1994 to mark the Society's fiftieth anniversary. The first World Vegan Day was held on November 1, 1994.

    Why is World Vegan Day on November 1?

    Because November is the month the Vegan Society was founded, in November 1944. The Society says November was chosen for exactly that reason.

    Who coined the word "vegan"?

    Donald Watson, one of the Society's six founding members, from a suggestion by early members George A. Henderson and Fay K. Henderson that the organization be called Allvega and its magazine Allvegan.

    Is World Vegan Day the same thing as World Vegan Month?

    They are the same lineage. The day came first, in 1994; the Society later extended it to World Vegan Week and then to World Vegan Month, which now covers all of November.
