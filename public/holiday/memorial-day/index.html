    Memorial Day

    Memorial Day falls on the last Monday in May and honors Americans who died in military service — the distinction from Veterans Day that a three-day weekend has done a good deal to blur. Its beginnings are genuinely disputed: at least five towns claim an observance earlier than the order that set the national date, and that order was issued by a veterans' organization rather than by the government.

    History

    The order everyone cites is General Orders No. 11, issued on May 5, 1868 by John A. Logan as commander-in-chief of the Grand Army of the Republic — a Union veterans' organization, not a federal agency. It designated May 30 for decorating the graves of comrades with flowers, a date chosen, the VA's National Cemetery Administration records, so that "the choicest flowers of springtime" would be in bloom nationwide, and it urged that the graves be guarded "with sacred vigilance." The first national observance followed at Arlington National Cemetery on May 30, 1868, where James A. Garfield — then a general and congressman, later president — gave remarks, alongside prayers, hymns, and a recitation of the Gettysburg Address and of the order itself.

    Logan did not invent the practice. The National Cemetery Administration records earlier claims: three women decorating graves at Boalsburg, Pennsylvania in October 1864; a program on May 1, 1865 at Charleston's Martyrs of the Race Course cemetery, where 257 Union dead were buried; Columbus, Mississippi on April 25, 1866, and Columbus, Georgia the following day. The Library of Congress adds Waterloo, New York, which President Lyndon Johnson proclaimed the birthplace of Memorial Day for an observance on May 5, 1866. Even the authorship of the order is contested — the Grand Army of the Republic credited an anonymous comrade's letter, while Logan's wife credited herself in her autobiography.

    The name drifted rather than changed. Neither the National Cemetery Administration nor the Library of Congress assigns a date to Decoration Day becoming Memorial Day, placing it only in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries.

    Timeline

    • 1865 — May 1: a memorial program is held at Charleston's Martyrs of the Race Course cemetery, the burial place of 257 Union dead.
    • 1866 — April 25 and 26: Columbus, Mississippi and Columbus, Georgia hold memorial observances a day apart, each claiming primacy.
    • 1868 — May 5: the Grand Army of the Republic issues General Orders No. 11, designating May 30 for decorating soldiers' graves with flowers.
    • 1868 — May 30: the first national observance is held at Arlington National Cemetery, with remarks by James A. Garfield.
    • 1968 — the Uniform Monday Holiday Act moves the observance from May 30 to the last Monday in May, effective in 1971.
    • 2000 — December: the National Moment of Remembrance Act, Public Law 106-579, creates the commission behind the 3 p.m. minute of silence.

    How to observe

    At 3 p.m. local time, stop. The National Moment of Remembrance Act, Public Law 106-579, signed in December 2000, created the commission that endorsed a minute of silence at that hour. It is the one part of the holiday with an exact time attached, and the part most widely overlooked.

    If you can get to Arlington, go in the days just before the weekend. The 3d U.S. Infantry Regiment — the Old Guard — places approximately 250,000 small American flags there, one at each headstone and along every row in the columbarium courts and niche walls, a tradition the cemetery dates to 1948, the year the regiment was designated the Army's official ceremonial unit. The flags are removed after Memorial Day, before the cemetery opens to the public.

    And the original instruction still works: decorate a grave. General Orders No. 11 was about flowers on particular graves, not about a weekend. A national cemetery will do, and so will the one veteran's grave in your own town.

    Frequently asked questions

    Is Memorial Day the same as Veterans Day?

    No. Memorial Day honors those who died in military service and began as a day for decorating their graves. Veterans Day, on November 11, honors everyone who served, living or dead.

    Why is Memorial Day on a Monday?

    The Uniform Monday Holiday Act, enacted in 1968 and effective in 1971, moved it from the fixed date of May 30 to the last Monday in May. A few states still mark May 30 as well.

    Who started Memorial Day?

    No one can say conclusively, and the reputable sources decline to. General Orders No. 11 of May 5, 1868 set the national date, but earlier observances are claimed by Boalsburg, Pennsylvania in October 1864, Charleston, South Carolina on May 1, 1865, Columbus, Mississippi and Columbus, Georgia in April 1866, and Waterloo, New York, which President Johnson proclaimed the holiday's birthplace. The VA and the Library of Congress record the claims without resolving them.

    Why was May 30 chosen?

    For the flowers. The order picked the date so that "the choicest flowers of springtime" would be available across the country.

    What is the National Moment of Remembrance?

    A minute of silence at 3 p.m. local time on Memorial Day, endorsed by the White House commission created by the National Moment of Remembrance Act, Public Law 106-579, in December 2000.
