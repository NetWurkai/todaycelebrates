    All Souls' Day falls on November 2 and is the Catholic Church's Commemoration of All the Faithful Departed: the day set aside to pray for the dead, as distinct from All Saints' Day the morning before, which honors the saints already in heaven. The practice of praying for the dead is far older than the date. The date itself is traceable to one Burgundian abbey at the end of the tenth century.

    History

    Christians were praying for their dead long before any calendar fixed a day for it. Vatican News points to evidence from the second century that Christians prayed and celebrated the Eucharist for their beloved dead, at first on the third day after burial and then on the anniversary, and later on the seventh and thirtieth days after a death.

    The November 2 date comes from one abbot and one monastery. "Remembering the dead on the 2 November became official in the year 998 when Abbot Odilo of Cluny (994-1048) made it obligatory in all the monasteries subject to him," Vatican News states. Cluny's network of houses was large, and the observance spread outward from it.

    The doctrine underneath the day is purgatory. The Catechism of the Catholic Church teaches that those who die in God's grace and friendship but still imperfectly purified "undergo purification, so as to achieve the holiness necessary to enter the joy of heaven" (1030), gives that final purification the name Purgatory (1031), and grounds the practice of prayer for the dead in scripture, quoting 2 Maccabees 12:46 (1032). The same paragraph commends almsgiving, indulgences and works of penance on behalf of the dead.

    Two later decisions shaped how the day is kept. In 1915, Pope Benedict XV granted priests permission to celebrate several Masses on November 2. And in the Table of Liturgical Days attached to the Universal Norms on the Liturgical Year, the Commemoration of All the Faithful Departed sits in the first and highest group, above the Sundays of Christmas Time and Ordinary Time in the second. Because the norms say the highest-ranked celebration is the one observed, a November 2 falling on an ordinary Sunday is kept as All Souls' Day.

    Timeline

    • 2nd century — Evidence that Christians prayed and celebrated the Eucharist for their dead, at first on the third day after burial and then on the anniversary of a death.
    • 998 — Abbot Odilo of Cluny makes the commemoration of the dead on November 2 obligatory in all the monasteries subject to him, fixing the date.
    • 1915 — Pope Benedict XV grants priests permission to celebrate several Masses on this day.
    • 1969 — The Universal Norms on the Liturgical Year, issued February 14, 1969 and in force from January 1, 1970, place the Commemoration of All the Faithful Departed in the highest group of the Table of Liturgical Days.

    How to observe

    For Catholics the plain observance is one of the day's Masses, and the day gives a parish unusual room: the 1915 permission lets a priest celebrate several Masses, so the schedule may be fuller than a normal weekday. Many parishes also keep a book or a list of the dead through November, and adding a name to it takes a minute and tends to be the part people remember doing years later.

    Visiting a grave is the older custom and needs no liturgy at all. Where the graves are too far to reach, the substitute that holds up is writing the names out by hand rather than thinking them.

    The three Catechism paragraphs that carry the whole doctrine, 1030 through 1032, take two minutes to read and are more specific than most summaries of them.

    Frequently asked questions

    What is the difference between All Saints' Day and All Souls' Day?

    All Saints' Day, on November 1, honors the saints. All Souls' Day, the next day, is the Commemoration of All the Faithful Departed, and its subject is prayer for those who have died and are still being purified. One looks at heaven; the other looks at the people still on their way there.

    What happens when November 2 falls on a Sunday?

    The Commemoration takes precedence over an ordinary Sunday. In the Table of Liturgical Days it sits in the first group, above the Sundays of Christmas Time and Ordinary Time in the second, and the norms say that when several celebrations fall on one day the highest-ranked is observed. The Sundays of Advent, Lent and Easter still outrank it.

    How many Masses can a priest celebrate on All Souls' Day?

    Vatican News says Pope Benedict XV granted permission in 1915 for priests to celebrate several Masses on this day. It says several, not a number. The figure of three is repeated widely but is not what that page states, so it is not claimed here.

    Why do Catholics pray for the dead?

    Because the Church teaches that some of the dead are still being purified and that prayer reaches them. The Catechism rests this on a practice already present in scripture, quoting 2 Maccabees 12:46, and says the Church has honored the memory of the dead and offered prayers for them, above all the Eucharistic sacrifice.

    Is All Souls' Day a federal holiday?

    No. Federal holidays are set by 5 U.S.C. 6103 and listed by the Office of Personnel Management, and November 2 is not among them.
