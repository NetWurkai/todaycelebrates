    Buy Nothing Day falls on the Friday after Thanksgiving in the United States and Canada, which is to say it is deliberately pinned to one of the busiest shopping days of the year. The ask is a single thing: twenty-four hours of buying nothing at all. It is one of the few days on the calendar built as an argument against the day it shares.

    History

    Buy Nothing Day was conceived in 1992 by the Canadian artist Ted Dave, and it became the first campaign carried by the Adbusters Media Foundation, a Vancouver non-profit that Simon Fraser University's archivists describe as anti-consumerism and anti-advertising. Adbusters itself dates to 1989, founded by Kalle Lasn and Bill Shmalz — SFU's spelling — after the major television networks refused to sell them air time for an anti-advertisement. Adbusters and Lasn, Britannica notes, went on to do much of the organizing and promotion that made the day travel.

    Credit for the idea is not recorded the same way everywhere, and it is worth saying so rather than smoothing it over. Britannica and SFU Archives both name Ted Dave as the person who conceived it. NPR's Morning Edition, in 2005, introduced Kalle Lasn flatly as "the creator of Buy Nothing Day." This page follows Britannica and SFU: SFU Archives acquired the Adbusters Media Foundation's own records in 2020, which makes it the nearer record of who did what.

    Two things frequently said about the day cannot be sourced here and are therefore left out: the month and location of the first observance, and the year the date moved to the Friday after Thanksgiving. Britannica establishes only that the day is now typically observed in North America on that Friday.

    How far it reaches is also better described than counted. Britannica records Buy Nothing Day campaigns appearing in Asia, Europe, South America and Australia. The figure usually quoted — more than 60 countries — comes from Lasn himself, in a 2017 interview with the Orlando Weekly, and no independent count appears in any of the sources used here.

    Timeline

    • 1989 — Adbusters is founded in Vancouver by Kalle Lasn and Bill Shmalz after the major networks refuse to sell them air time for an anti-advertisement.
    • 1992 — The Canadian artist Ted Dave conceives Buy Nothing Day; it becomes the first campaign Adbusters launches.
    • 1997 — An early Buy Nothing Day poster is produced; the copy is now held in SFU's Adbusters Media Foundation fonds.
    • 2005 — Lasn describes the day on NPR's Morning Edition as a personal twenty-four-hour consumer fast.
    • 2017 — Lasn tells the Orlando Weekly the day now reaches "more than 60 countries," a figure no independent count confirms.
    • 2020 — Simon Fraser University Archives acquires the Adbusters Media Foundation's records, covering its campaigns over more than three decades.

    How to observe

    Treat it as logistics rather than willpower. The people who find the day easy are the ones who bought groceries on Thursday; the people who find it impossible are generally discovering that an ordinary Friday has four or five purchases built into it before lunch. Noticing which ones is most of the exercise.

    Lasn's own framing is narrower than a boycott. He described it on NPR in 2005 as "a very personal day" and a twenty-four-hour consumer fast, not a campaign against a particular store or brand, and his argument was broader than one day: "I think that we can solve our ecological and our psychological and our political problems by just starting to consume less."

    If the history interests you more than the fast, the primary record is open. Simon Fraser University Archives holds the Adbusters Media Foundation fonds, acquired in 2020, which documents the organization's campaigns across more than three decades and includes Buy Nothing Day material going back to the 1990s — among it a Buy Nothing Day poster from 1997. That is the actual paper trail, rather than the retelling.

    Frequently asked questions

    When is Buy Nothing Day?

    In North America it is observed on the Friday following Thanksgiving Day in the United States, which falls on November 27 in 2026.

    Who started Buy Nothing Day?

    Britannica and Simon Fraser University Archives both credit the Canadian artist Ted Dave, who conceived it in 1992. NPR's Morning Edition in 2005 instead called Kalle Lasn, the Adbusters co-founder who promoted the day, "the creator of Buy Nothing Day." The two accounts have not been reconciled; this page follows Britannica and SFU, since SFU holds Adbusters' own records.

    Is Buy Nothing Day run by an organization?

    It has no governing body, but the Adbusters Media Foundation of Vancouver — which launched it as its first campaign and whose co-founder Kalle Lasn promoted it for decades — has done most of the organizing and promotion.

    Is it observed outside North America?

    Yes. Britannica records Buy Nothing Day campaigns in Asia, Europe, South America and Australia. Lasn claimed more than 60 countries in 2017, but that number comes from him rather than from any independent count.

    What are you supposed to do on Buy Nothing Day?

    Buy nothing for twenty-four hours. Lasn has described it as a personal consumer fast rather than a boycott of any particular company.
