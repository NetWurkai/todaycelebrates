    World Diabetes Day falls on November 14, the birthday of Frederick Banting, who shared the 1923 Nobel Prize for the work that produced insulin. It is one of the few observances with a complete paper trail: the International Diabetes Federation began marking the date in 1991, and the United Nations General Assembly designated it a United Nations Day by resolution in December 2006. The campaign exists because the disease keeps outrunning the response to it.

    History

    The date belongs to Frederick Grant Banting, born November 14, 1891, at Alliston, Ontario. He shared the 1923 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine with J. J. R. Macleod; the International Diabetes Federation credits him with co-discovering insulin with Charles Best in 1922. Banting died on February 21, 1941.

    The observance is much younger, and the two organizations behind it describe their own roles differently. The IDF's site says the day was created in 1991 by the IDF and the World Health Organization. WHO says it was established in 1991 by the IDF with WHO support. The United Nations resolution that later adopted the day settles the question in WHO's favor, recording that the IDF had been observing November 14 at a global level since 1991 with WHO co-sponsorship.

    That resolution is the day's legal footing, and its dates are widely reported wrong. The General Assembly adopted resolution 61/225 on December 20, 2006, at its 83rd plenary meeting. It decides to designate 14 November, the current World Diabetes Day, as a United Nations Day, to be observed every year beginning in 2007. The designation is therefore from 2006 and the first UN observance from 2007 — and the UN's own observance page, which gives 2007 as the year of adoption, contradicts the resolution it is describing.

    Timeline

    • November 14, 1891 — Frederick Grant Banting is born at Alliston, Ontario.
    • 1923 — Banting shares the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine with J. J. R. Macleod.
    • 1991 — The International Diabetes Federation begins observing November 14 as World Diabetes Day, with co-sponsorship by the World Health Organization.
    • December 20, 2006 — The UN General Assembly adopts resolution 61/225 at its 83rd plenary meeting, designating November 14 a United Nations Day.
    • 2007 — The first observance as a United Nations Day, as the resolution directs.
    • 2022 — WHO records 830 million people living with diabetes worldwide, up from 200 million in 1990.

    How to observe

    Get tested if you have not been. The IDF puts the number of people living with undiagnosed diabetes at 252 million, which is the single statistic the day is built around.

    If someone in your household already has a diagnosis, check that the treatment is actually happening. WHO found that 59 percent of adults aged 30 and over living with diabetes were not taking medication for it in 2022. The gap between diagnosis and treatment is larger than most people assume, and it is the part a family member can see.

    Read the WHO diabetes fact sheet rather than campaign material. It carries the figures, the trend since 1990 and the mortality data in about two screens, without asking anything of you.

    WHO lists the 2026 theme as early action on diabetes, dated November 14, 2026.

    Frequently asked questions

    Why is World Diabetes Day on November 14?

    It is Frederick Banting's birthday. He was born on November 14, 1891, and shared the 1923 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine for the work behind insulin.

    Is World Diabetes Day a United Nations day?

    Yes. The General Assembly adopted resolution 61/225 on December 20, 2006, designating November 14 a United Nations Day to be observed every year beginning in 2007.

    Who created it?

    The International Diabetes Federation, in 1991. The IDF's own site describes the day as created jointly with the World Health Organization; WHO describes itself as having supported an IDF campaign, and the UN resolution uses the word co-sponsorship.

    How many people have diabetes?

    The two bodies behind the day publish different totals. WHO's fact sheet gives 830 million people in 2022, up from 200 million in 1990. The IDF's site gives 589 million. The figures are not interchangeable, they are not reconciled by either organization's page, and neither total is an update of the other.

    Is November 14 a day off?

    No. World Diabetes Day is not a federal holiday in the United States, and nothing closes for it.
