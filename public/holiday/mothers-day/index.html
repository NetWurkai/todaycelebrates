    Celebration

    Mother's Day falls on the second Sunday in May, a date Congress fixed in 1914, and it was the work of one woman who spent much of the rest of her life objecting to what it became. Anna Jarvis built it as a memorial to her own mother and insisted on the singular possessive: one mother, your mother, not a general tribute to motherhood. The apostrophe held. Almost nothing else she wanted did.

    History

    Unusually for an American holiday, this one has a documented founder and a documented first day. Anna Maria Jarvis was born in Webster, West Virginia, on May 1, 1864. Her mother, Ann Reeves Jarvis, had organized Mothers' Day Work Clubs that cared for sick and wounded soldiers from both sides during the Civil War, and afterward arranged a Mother's Friendship Day meant to bring together the mothers of Union and Confederate veterans. When Ann died in 1905, her daughter began campaigning for a national day in her memory.

    The first formal Mother's Day service was held on May 10, 1908, at Andrews Methodist Episcopal Church in Grafton, West Virginia. Jarvis herself was not there — she was at a Mother's Day event at Wanamaker's in Philadelphia — but she sent 500 white carnations for the congregation. Philadelphia newspaper publishers promoted the second Sunday in May, West Virginia passed a law recognizing the day in 1910, and other states followed.

    Congress acted on May 8, 1914, with a joint resolution, H.J. Res. 263, entered in the statutes as Public Resolution No. 25 and titled "Designating the second Sunday in May as Mother's Day, and for other purposes." Woodrow Wilson issued the proclamation the next day, asking Americans to display the flag in honor of mothers whose sons had died in war.

    What followed is the part Jarvis is least remembered for. She spent her later years opposing the holiday's commercial turn, objecting to florists who raised carnation prices for the occasion and to standardized greeting cards, which she regarded as a way of not writing to your mother. She died on November 24, 1948, in West Chester, Pennsylvania, at 84, having outlived her own holiday's original form by decades.

    How to observe

    Write something. Jarvis's specific complaint about printed cards was that buying one is a way of avoiding the harder thing, and she is the person who built the holiday, so the objection carries some weight.

    Find out about the woman the day is actually for. The original Mother's Day was a memorial service for one person, held by a daughter who had lost her, and the questions you can only ask while someone is alive are the ones the holiday was invented around.

    The Grafton church where that first service was held still stands as the International Mother's Day Shrine; the National Park Service documents its place in the holiday's history and the Library of Congress holds photographs of it.

    And if you want the holiday's arrival as it was actually reported, the Library of Congress's Chronicling America guide to Mother's Day collects newspaper coverage from 1908 onward. The years between 1908 and 1914 show a holiday being argued into existence rather than handed down.

    Frequently asked questions

    Why is it "Mother's Day" and not "Mothers' Day"?

    Because the singular possessive was the point. Jarvis meant a day for each person's own mother rather than a tribute to mothers in general, and the 1914 joint resolution uses that spelling in its title.

    Is Mother's Day a federal holiday?

    No. Federal holidays are set by law, under 5 U.S.C. 6103, and Mother's Day is not among them. The 1914 resolution designated the day and asked for the flag to be displayed; it did not create a day off.

    Who founded Mother's Day?

    Anna Maria Jarvis, born in Webster, West Virginia, on May 1, 1864. She began campaigning after her mother's death in 1905, the first service was held in 1908, and Congress designated the day in 1914.

    Did the founder really turn against the holiday?

    Yes. The National Park Service records her objecting to florists raising carnation prices for the occasion and to standardized greeting cards.

    Is Mother's Day the same date everywhere?

    No. Many countries observe it on the American date, the second Sunday in May, while others mark a day for mothers at a different time of year entirely, with separate origins.
