    World Peace Day turns up on November 17 in most American day-of-the-year listings, and no founding organization, government page, or news report explains how it got there. The two peace observances that do have documented origins fall elsewhere on the calendar: January 1, proposed by Pope Paul VI in December 1967, and September 21, established by the United Nations General Assembly. This page gives what the record supports and leaves the November 17 date where the evidence leaves it.

    History

    The origin of this observance is not well documented. No founding body claims November 17, no presidential proclamation or United Nations resolution designates it, and the pages that promote the date are daily-holiday aggregators and retail blogs — the kind of source this site does not cite, because they are where unsourced claims circulate. What follows is the part that can be shown: there are two world peace days with real origins, and neither of them is on November 17.

    The older one belongs to the Catholic Church. On 8 December 1967, Pope Paul VI issued a message proposing "to celebrate 'The Day of Peace', throughout the world, on the first day of the year, January 1, 1968," and added that it was his desire "that then, every year, this commemoration be repeated as a hope and as a promise." The Vatican's own numbering treats the 1968 observance as the first in that series, and the day is kept on January 1.

    The second is the United Nations' International Day of Peace, established in 1981 by General Assembly resolution 36/67. Its date was not originally fixed. The day was timed to coincide with the opening of the General Assembly's annual session, which was then held on the third Tuesday of September, so it moved from year to year. The first observance was held in September 1982. In 2001 the General Assembly unanimously adopted resolution 55/282, which established 21 September as an annual day of non-violence and cease-fire — a fixed date replacing a floating one, which is the reverse of the direction these things usually travel.

    Neither of those dates has ever moved to November, and nothing in the published record connects November 17 to either of them.

    Timeline

    • 8 December 1967 — Pope Paul VI issues a message proposing that a "Day of Peace" be celebrated throughout the world on January 1, 1968, and asks that the commemoration be repeated every year.
    • 1 January 1968 — The date named in that message; the Vatican lists this as the first World Day of Peace.
    • 1981 — The United Nations General Assembly establishes the International Day of Peace by resolution 36/67, timed to coincide with the opening of its annual session, then held on the third Tuesday of September.
    • September 1982 — The first UN Peace Day is observed.
    • 2001 — The General Assembly, by unanimous vote, adopts resolution 55/282, establishing 21 September as an annual day of non-violence and cease-fire.

    How to observe

    Read the 1967 message. It is short, it is online in English on the Vatican's site, and its argument — that peace is a condition to be built and defended rather than wished for — is more specific than most of what has been written about it since.

    Read the two General Assembly resolutions, 36/67 and 55/282. The UN's own observance page links to both. The second is worth reading next to the first, because it shows a floating observance being pinned to a date, with the reasoning on the page.

    Then mark the day that is actually being observed. The United Nations publishes a theme for 21 September each year — for 2026 it is "Invest in Peace - For Everyone, Everywhere, Every Day" — along with programming anyone can follow from its observance page.

    And if you arrived here from a calendar that lists World Peace Day on November 17, check whether that calendar cites anyone for the date. The answer is the useful part of the exercise.

    Frequently asked questions

    Is World Peace Day on November 17 an official observance?

    No. There is no presidential proclamation, no United Nations resolution, and no founding organization behind the November 17 date. It appears on commercial day-of-the-year calendars, none of which cite a source for it.

    What is the difference between World Peace Day and the International Day of Peace?

    The International Day of Peace is the United Nations observance, held on 21 September and established by General Assembly resolution 36/67 in 1981. "World Peace Day" is used loosely for that day, for the Catholic Church's January 1 World Day of Peace, and — on American calendars — for November 17.

    Who started the January 1 World Day of Peace?

    Pope Paul VI, in a message dated 8 December 1967 that proposed celebrating a Day of Peace throughout the world on January 1, 1968, and asked that it be repeated annually.

    Why was the UN's peace day originally on a different date each year?

    Because resolution 36/67 tied it to the opening of the General Assembly's annual session, which was then held on the third Tuesday of September. Resolution 55/282, adopted in 2001, fixed the observance on 21 September.

    Does anything else fall on November 17?

    Yes. Several observances share the date, including International Students' Day. None of them accounts for a World Peace Day on November 17.
