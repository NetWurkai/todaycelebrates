    National Zinfandel Day celebrates the grape American wine spent a century and a half treating as its own, until DNA tests placed its origin on the Croatian coast. The observance is held on the third Wednesday of November, which in 2026 is November 18. Its own history is already muddled: the organization behind it announced a first Zinfandel Day for November 19, 2010, then later wrote that it created National Zinfandel Day in 2012.

    History

    Zinfandel Advocates & Producers, known as ZAP, is the one body that claims the day. It describes itself as "a 501(c)(3) nonprofit association which organizes seminars, educational programs, and tastings," founded in 1991 with Jerry Seps as its first president. Its two accounts of the holiday do not agree.

    The earlier and more primary one is a press release issued over ZAP's name on November 5, 2010, datelined Rough & Ready, California, and headlined "First Annual #Zinfandel Day November 19." The text announces "Global Zinfandel Day (called '#Zinfandel'), November 19th from 12:01 a.m. to 11:59 p.m. (PST)" - a 24-hour online tasting event put together with "blogger and social media entrepreneur R. Christian Oggenfuss," with ZAP's then executive director Rebecca Robinson quoted in it. The release never uses the phrase "National Zinfandel Day," and November 19, 2010 was a Friday.

    A ZAP blog post of September 28, 2021 tells it differently: "Back in 2012, the Zinfandel Advocates & Producers (ZAP) team created National Zinfandel Day," observed "on the third Wednesday of every November." Decanter repeats both the 2012 date and the third-Wednesday rule. The conflict is stated here rather than averaged: the 2010 release is a dated document from the organization itself, the 2012 claim a recollection published eleven years later, so the first event most likely took place in 2010 under a different name.

    The grape's history is better documented than the holiday's. Zinfandel's identity was an open question for most of the twentieth century. In 1967 a United States Department of Agriculture researcher based at UC Davis noticed that Puglia's Primitivo resembled California Zinfandel; ZAP's own history names him as the plant pathologist Austin Goheen, while the UC Davis account leaves him unnamed. The answer came from Croatia. UC Davis grapevine geneticist Carole Meredith, working with Ivan Pejic and Edi Maletic of the University of Zagreb, located a Dalmatian variety called Crljenak Kastelanski in September 2000, and DNA tests in Meredith's lab in December 2001 confirmed that Crljenak, Zinfandel and Primitivo are one variety. ZAP adds, without citing a source, that only nine vines were found.

    Timeline

    • 1967 - A USDA researcher based at UC Davis notices that Puglia's Primitivo resembles California Zinfandel.
    • 1991 - Zinfandel Advocates & Producers is founded as an educational 501(c)(3), with Jerry Seps as its first president.
    • September 2000 - Crljenak Kastelanski is located in a vineyard on Croatia's Dalmatian coast.
    • December 2001 - DNA tests in Carole Meredith's UC Davis lab confirm that Crljenak, Zinfandel and Primitivo are the same variety.
    • November 19, 2010 - ZAP and R. Christian Oggenfuss hold what their press release calls the first annual Zinfandel Day, a 24-hour online event.
    • 2012 - Writing in 2021, ZAP dates its creation of National Zinfandel Day to this year, on the third Wednesday of November.

    How to observe

    Pour a California Zinfandel next to a Primitivo from Puglia. They are the same grape grown a world apart, which is the whole point of the 2001 finding.

    Look for old-vine bottlings and field blends rather than the ripest thing on the shelf. ZAP funds a Heritage Vineyard project with UC Davis to collect and study old Zinfandel selections.

    Check the date before planning anything. The observance moves with the third Wednesday, so it was November 17 in 2021 and November 18 in 2026. ZAP's own tasting event, Zinfandel Experience, is separate: the organization lists its 2027 signature weekend in Healdsburg on January 29-30.

    Frequently asked questions

    When is National Zinfandel Day?

    The third Wednesday of November, which in 2026 is November 18. The date moves with the weekday: ZAP gives November 17 for 2021, and the first event was held on November 19, 2010.

    Who created National Zinfandel Day?

    Zinfandel Advocates & Producers, a California nonprofit founded in 1991. ZAP's 2010 press release announces a first "Global Zinfandel Day" organized with the blogger R. Christian Oggenfuss; a 2021 post from the same organization says the ZAP team created National Zinfandel Day in 2012. Both accounts are ZAP's, and both are printed here.

    Is Zinfandel an American grape?

    It is an American specialty, not an American native. DNA tests at UC Davis confirmed in December 2001 that Zinfandel is identical to Crljenak Kastelanski, a variety from Croatia's Dalmatian coast, also known there as Tribidrag.

    Is Primitivo the same thing as Zinfandel?

    Genetically, yes: same variety, different names and different growing traditions. A USDA researcher at UC Davis raised the possibility in 1967, and the 2001 DNA work settled it.

    How much Zinfandel is grown in California?

    ZAP says more than 39,000 acres, and calls Zinfandel the third most crushed wine grape in California after Cabernet Sauvignon and Chardonnay. Neither figure carries a year or a source on its page, so both appear here as the association's own claim. The federal acreage and crush reports that would confirm them could not be retrieved.
