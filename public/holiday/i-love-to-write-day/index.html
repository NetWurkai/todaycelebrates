    I Love to Write Day falls on November 15 and asks people of any age to spend part of the day writing something: a poem, a letter, the first page of a novel. It was created in 2002 by John Riddle, an author in Bear, Delaware, and it is unusually well attested for a grassroots observance: the founder is still at it, still explaining it on the record, and candid that he picked the date for no reason at all.

    History

    Riddle has told the story more than once, and consistently. On the Delaware Division of the Arts' State of the Arts podcast in November 2025 he dated the idea to mid-May 2002, on a drive from Delaware to a writers' conference in Asheville, North Carolina, somewhere past Richmond. He launched the campaign that year and attached it to November 15. Asked why that date, he said he still does not know: he picked it out of the blue. No source offers another explanation, which is worth saying plainly — observances of this kind usually acquire a tidy reason after the fact.

    What the day asks is deliberately small. In a letter to the editor published in the Delaware State News on November 15, 2022, Riddle described a grassroots campaign "to have people of all ages spend time writing every Nov. 15," and listed the options as a poem, a love letter, an essay, starting a novel or finishing one. In a June 2026 op-ed for the same paper he put it as having "officially declared each Nov. 15 to be a day dedicated to encouraging people of all ages to write."

    The scale claims are his own, and this page reports them as his rather than as established figures. Riddle has written that more than 11,000 schools across the country signed up for the first observance; that the governors of nine states, Delaware among them, have proclaimed November 15 as I Love to Write Day; and that the day was entered into the Congressional Record in 2008 by Senator Tom Carper of Delaware. None of the three could be confirmed against an independent source for this page, and the nine states are never named.

    Riddle is a full-time freelance writer and ghostwriter who has worked from Delaware since 1996, and he keeps a public count of his books: in November 2025 his fortieth had just come out, a humor title written with two grandsons; by June 2026 he put the total at 42. The campaign's domain, ilovetowriteday.org, now serves an empty default WordPress page, so the day's organizing presence is, in practice, Riddle himself.

    How to observe

    Pick a form with a built-in ending. The day is one day, and what makes it work is finishing something: a letter, a one-page memory, a list poem, a note to someone who will be surprised to get it. An opening chapter abandoned on November 16 is the failure mode.

    Write to someone rather than for an audience. Riddle's own list puts a love letter next to an essay for a reason, and the easiest writing to finish is the kind with a recipient.

    If you want the founder's version rather than a secondhand one, the Delaware Division of the Arts published a full episode and transcript with him in November 2025. It is free, it comes from a state arts agency, and it is the fullest account this page could find.

    Frequently asked questions

    Who started I Love to Write Day?

    John Riddle, an author living in Bear, Delaware, in 2002. He has given that year himself in print and on a Delaware state arts agency podcast, and no source gives a different one.

    Why is it on November 15?

    There is no reason. Riddle has said on the record that he does not know why he chose the date — it came to him out of the blue — and he has never offered another account.

    Is it an official holiday?

    No. The date carries no federal or state legal status. Riddle says the governors of nine states have issued proclamations and that the day was entered into the Congressional Record in 2008, but those are his own statements and neither could be independently confirmed for this page.

    What are you supposed to write?

    Anything. The founder's own list is a poem, a love letter, an essay, starting a novel or finishing one. The campaign is pitched at people of all ages, not at working writers.

    Is there an official website?

    Not a working one. The domain ilovetowriteday.org currently returns an empty default WordPress page with no content about the observance. Riddle promotes the day himself, through op-eds, letters and interviews.
