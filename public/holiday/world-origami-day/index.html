    November 11 is Origami Day in Japan, a date the Nippon Origami Association settled on in 1980, and it is also the closing day of World Origami Days, the folding season OrigamiUSA runs from October 24. The date was chosen for its arithmetic: written 11/11, it is four ones, read as the four sides of a square sheet of paper.

    History

    The Japanese half of this day is the well-sourced half. The Nippon Origami Association, founded in October 1973, states on its own site that in 1980 this day was designated Origami Day, and gives two reasons for the date. The four ones in 11/11 stand for the four sides of a square sheet of origami paper. And November 11 is the anniversary of the 1918 armistice that ended the First World War, marked in Japan as World Peace Memorial Day. The association's sentence names no subject, so strictly it says the day was designated rather than who designated it; a Japanese government publication credits the association directly.

    The American half grew out of a birthday. OrigamiUSA records that in September 2005 it floated October 24 as a special origami day, that being the birthday of Lillian Oppenheimer (1898-1992). Makoto Yamaguchi of Origami House pointed out that Japan already had one on November 11. Rather than choose between them, OrigamiUSA proposed treating the whole stretch from October 24 to November 11 as World Origami Days, which is how both OrigamiUSA and the Japan Origami Academic Society publish it now. A committee for the season followed, and in 2008 a second one was formed to build it a website.

    Who Oppenheimer was is where the sources stop agreeing. OrigamiUSA's own article on the season calls her a co-founder of OrigamiUSA as well as of the British Origami Society. A Japanese government publication says she was charmed by paper folding around 1953, worked from an Origami Center she founded in 1958, and that OrigamiUSA was founded only after she died. OrigamiUSA's own history page gives no founding year for her center: it calls her the founder of organized origami in America, says Michael Shall and others founded The Friends of The Origami Center of America in the 1970s, that The Friends incorporated in 1980, bought her supplies business in 1987, and became OrigamiUSA effective July 1, 1994. On the organization's own dates this page follows the history page. The 1958 founding of her center rests on the government article alone.

    Timeline

    • 1898 — Lillian Oppenheimer is born. OrigamiUSA later calls her the founder of organized origami in America.
    • October 1973 — The Nippon Origami Association is founded in Japan.
    • 1980 — November 11 is designated Origami Day, per the Nippon Origami Association's own site. In the same year The Friends of The Origami Center of America incorporates in the United States.
    • 1987 — The Friends buys Oppenheimer's origami supplies business. She dies in 1992.
    • July 1, 1994 — The Friends of The Origami Center of America becomes OrigamiUSA.
    • September 2005 — OrigamiUSA proposes October 24 as an origami day, learns Japan already observes November 11, and proposes the span between the two as World Origami Days.

    How to observe

    Fold somewhere public. OrigamiUSA's own suggestion for the season is as unglamorous as it is effective: fold on the bus, in line at the bank, at the library, and give the result away. A model handed to a stranger does more for the craft than a model photographed.

    Enter something. The Japan Origami Academic Society runs an online exhibition across the season, judged for its Kawasaki Award; the 2026 edition took origami horses, with registration opening September 15 and submissions open from October 24 through November 11.

    Learn one model from a printed diagram instead of a video. Diagrams are what origami is actually built on, and the symbol set is standard enough that a Japanese diagram works without a word of Japanese. If the armistice reading of the date appeals to you, that is the tradition the association itself cites, not a later gloss.

    Frequently asked questions

    Is World Origami Day on November 11 or October 24?

    Both belong to the same season. OrigamiUSA runs World Origami Days from October 24, Lillian Oppenheimer's birthday, through November 11, which is Origami Day in Japan. November 11 is the closing day.

    Why was November 11 chosen?

    The Nippon Origami Association gives two reasons: the four ones in 11/11 represent the four sides of a square sheet of origami paper, and November 11 is the anniversary of the 1918 armistice.

    Who designated Origami Day in Japan?

    The 1980 designation appears on the Nippon Origami Association's own site, and a Japanese government publication credits the association by name. The association's own sentence does not identify who did it.

    Did Lillian Oppenheimer found OrigamiUSA?

    The sources disagree. One OrigamiUSA article calls her a co-founder, while OrigamiUSA's own history page describes the organization as the renamed Friends of The Origami Center of America, founded by Michael Shall and others, and a Japanese government publication says OrigamiUSA was founded after her death in 1992.

    Is there anything organized to take part in?

    Yes. The Japan Origami Academic Society runs an online exhibition and the Kawasaki Award across the season, and OrigamiUSA publishes a calendar of events and asks people to share photographs of what they fold.
