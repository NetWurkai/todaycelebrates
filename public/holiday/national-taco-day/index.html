    National Taco Day is listed on October 4 on most American calendars, but since 2024 the observance has officially sat on the first Tuesday in October — a change Taco Bell campaigned for and announced jointly with the calendar registry that lists it. The day has no verifiable founder and no founding year that survives checking, which is an odd position for a holiday attached to one of the most thoroughly researched foods in North America. The taco's history is the solid part; the holiday's is not.

    History

    The taco's history is well documented. The holiday's is not. No founder, organization, or founding year for National Taco Day can be confirmed from a primary source. The fullest account available is Taco Bell's own, published while the company was campaigning to move the date: it says several unnamed businesses settled on October 4 in 2009, and that the date was established on the National Day Calendar by 2013. The same page credits an earlier, locally observed San Antonio version in the 1960s to Roberto L. Gomez, attributing that history to the journalist Gustavo Arellano. All of it is one interested party's telling.

    The food is firmer ground. The historian Jeffrey M. Pilcher traced "taco" through eighteenth-century Spanish dictionaries, where it meant a ramrod, a billiard cue, or a carpenter's hammer; the Spanish Royal Academy extended it to a snack of food only in the mid-nineteenth century. Manuel Payno's 1891 novel Los bandidos de Río Frío carries what Pilcher calls perhaps the first unequivocal reference to the Mexican taco. The pre-fried hard shell, by contrast, is an American industrial object with a paper trail: Juvencio Maldonado, a New York restaurateur, filed for a frying form in 1947 and received US patent 2,506,305 in 1950.

    Timeline

    • 1891 — Manuel Payno's novel Los bandidos de Río Frío carries what Pilcher calls perhaps the first unequivocal reference to the Mexican taco.
    • 1914 — Bertha Haffner-Ginger, a Los Angeles Times columnist, publishes a recipe for tacos dorados.
    • July 21, 1947 — Juvencio Maldonado files a patent application for a form that folds and fries tortillas into taco shells.
    • May 2, 1950 — That patent, US 2,506,305, is granted.
    • 1962 — The first Taco Bell opens in Downey, California.
    • September 17, 2024 — Taco Bell and National Day Calendar announce that National Taco Day moves to the first Tuesday in October, shifting it that year from Friday, October 4 to Tuesday, October 1.

    How to observe

    Check which date your local restaurants are actually honoring. Since 2024 the observance has officially fallen on the first Tuesday in October, but October 4 is still printed on most calendars and still drives the promotions, so the two run in parallel and a deal advertised for National Taco Day may be either.

    Read the Maldonado patent. It is short, public, and free, and it is the clearest single artifact of how the hard-shell taco became a mass-produced American thing: a hinged metal form that folds and locks several tortillas at once so they can be dropped into hot fat together. The drawings explain the shape of nearly every supermarket taco shell sold since.

    If you want the argument rather than the object, Pilcher's 2008 article in Gastronomica asks whether the taco was invented in Southern California, and answers carefully enough to be worth the hour.

    Frequently asked questions

    Is National Taco Day on October 4 or the first Tuesday in October?

    Both dates are in use. October 4 was the long-standing date; on September 17, 2024, Taco Bell and National Day Calendar announced the move to the first Tuesday in October, which that year meant October 1. Most calendars, including this one, still list October 4.

    Who created National Taco Day?

    Not documented. No founder or founding year can be confirmed from a primary source. Taco Bell's own account names several unnamed businesses adopting October 4 in 2009, and an earlier 1960s San Antonio observance credited to Roberto L. Gomez, which it attributes to the journalist Gustavo Arellano.

    Why did Taco Bell want the date changed?

    The company's stated reason, from its chief marketing officer Taylor Montgomery, is that "it felt unnatural for it to not fall on a Tuesday." Taco Bell says the day had landed on a Tuesday only four times since 2000.

    Who invented the hard-shell taco?

    The pre-fried U-shaped shell has a patent record. Juvencio Maldonado, a New York restaurateur, filed in July 1947 and received US patent 2,506,305 in May 1950. Glen Bell, Taco Bell's founder, claimed to have invented the fast-food taco and built a taco fryer in 1951; Pilcher reports that Bell was apparently unaware Maldonado had reached the patent office first.

    Is National Taco Day a federal holiday?

    No. It has no official status. Nothing closes, and no one gets the day off.
