    Valentine's Day

    Send viaEmailTextFacebookXWhatsApp
    Date
    Sunday, February 14, 2027
    Recurs
    Every year on February 14
    Category
    Celebration

    Valentine's Day falls on February 14 and is, in the United States, a largely commercial occasion with a genuinely obscure past. The Library of Congress calls its origins "murky," and the honest version of the history is a series of plausible connections — a Roman fertility festival, one or more martyrs named Valentine, a Chaucer poem about birds choosing mates — rather than a line of descent anyone can trace end to end.

    History

    There were several early Christian martyrs named Valentine, and February 14 is the saint day associated with them; which one the day commemorates is not settled. The first known printed mention of Saint Valentine is believed to be in the Nuremberg Chronicle of 1493, and it says nothing about any role as a patron saint of lovers.

    The Roman connection is the one repeated most often and established least firmly. Romans held the feast of Lupercalia, a mid-February spring festival with fertility rites, on February 15. Pope Gelasius I banned Lupercalia in the late fifth century and is sometimes credited with replacing it with St. Valentine's Day — but that is an attribution rather than a documented act, and Britannica presents it as uncertain.

    Romance attaches to the date in the fourteenth century, and the earliest link anyone can point to is literary. Geoffrey Chaucer, who died in 1400, wrote in Parliament of Fowls of birds gathering in early spring on "seynt valentynes day" to choose their mates, which tracks a medieval belief that birds paired off on February 14. The oldest surviving valentine letter in English is later and more human: in February 1477 Margery Brews wrote to John Paston and signed herself "your Valentine, Margery Brews."

    Handmade valentines appear in the sixteenth century, probably the first greeting cards of any kind, and mass production begins as early as 1800. The American industry has a name and an address. Esther Howland was born in Worcester, Massachusetts in 1828, graduated from Mount Holyoke Female Seminary in 1847, and received her first English valentine that same year. Working from a third-floor room of the family house on Summer Street, she had the business fully launched by the end of 1849. Her brother Allen took samples out expecting perhaps $200 in orders and came back with $5,000 worth. Her first advertisement ran in the Worcester Daily Spy in February 1850, with simple cards at five cents and elaborate ones reaching a dollar. Sales eventually grew to $100,000 a year. She incorporated as the New England Valentine Company in the early 1870s, moved into a Main Street factory in 1879, and sold the business in 1880 to care for her ailing father. A newspaper later called her the Mother of the American Valentine.

    How to observe

    Read the Paston letter. Margery Brews's February 1477 note to John Paston survives in the British Library's Paston collection and is the earliest known valentine in English. It is also partly a negotiation about her dowry, which makes it a better document than a sentimental one.

    Look at Esther Howland's actual cards. The American Antiquarian Society in Worcester holds her valentines alongside the Victorian cards around them, and the layered paper-lace construction explains at a glance why a hand-assembled card could cost a dollar in 1850.

    Treat the spending figures as what they are. The National Retail Federation has surveyed Valentine's Day intentions annually since 2004 and put expected 2026 spending at a record $29.1 billion. That is a retail trade association's survey of what consumers say they plan to spend, not a government measurement of what they spent — worth citing, worth labeling.

    Frequently asked questions

    Who was St. Valentine?

    Not one identifiable person. February 14 is the saint day associated with several early Christian martyrs named Valentine, and the record does not say which of them the day commemorates. The earliest known printed mention of him, in the 1493 Nuremberg Chronicle, says nothing about lovers.

    Did Valentine's Day come from the Roman festival Lupercalia?

    Possibly, but it is not documented. Lupercalia fell on February 15, and Pope Gelasius I banned it in the late fifth century; he is sometimes credited with replacing it with St. Valentine's Day. Britannica treats that as an attribution rather than a fact, and the Library of Congress calls the holiday's origins murky.

    When did February 14 become about romance?

    In the fourteenth century. The earliest surviving link is Chaucer's Parliament of Fowls, in which birds choose their mates on "seynt valentynes day" — reflecting a medieval belief that birds paired off on that date.

    Who made the first American valentines?

    Esther Howland of Worcester, Massachusetts built the first American valentine business, launched by the end of 1849 and advertising from February 1850. Handmade valentines in Europe date to the sixteenth century and mass production began as early as 1800, so she was not first in the world — she was first at scale in the United States.

    Is Valentine's Day a federal holiday?

    No. It has no official status and nothing closes for it. In 2027 it falls on a Sunday.
