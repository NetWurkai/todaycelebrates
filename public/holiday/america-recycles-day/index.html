    America Recycles Day falls on November 15 and is the one day on the American calendar given over entirely to recycling. It began as a state campaign in Texas in 1994 and went national three years later; Keep America Beautiful runs it now, with the Environmental Protection Agency as its most visible federal backer. The premise is less celebration than arithmetic — the most recent federal figures show the national recycling rate going down, not up.

    History

    America Recycles Day started in one state. An account published by the U.S. Chamber of Commerce Foundation traces it to Texas Recycles Day, which began in 1994 as the idea of two employees of the Texas Commission on Environmental Quality, Kevin Tuerff and Valerie Davis, who chose November 15 for it. The pair later started an Austin environmental marketing firm and proposed taking the Texas event national, presenting the concept at the National Recycling Coalition's annual congress, where the same account says it was embraced immediately.

    The first national observance, by that account, was November 15, 1997, pre-announced by the NRC at its sixteenth congress in Orlando and launched at a Washington press conference on October 1, 1997 co-sponsored by the NRC and the EPA, with Vice President Al Gore as honorary chair. At least 40 states took part.

    That founding year does not quite agree with the EPA's own arithmetic, and this site does not average dates. The EPA's 2015 news release calls that year the 18th America Recycles Day, and its 2017 release marks the 20th — both of which count back to 1998 rather than 1997. The fullest published account gives 1997; the EPA's own numbering implies 1998; neither is reconciled here.

    Keep America Beautiful has been the day's managing organization since 2009, per the same Chamber Foundation account, and runs it today — expanding it for 2026, when its own page announces that America Recycles Day becomes America Recycles Month, with a daily challenge through November. Presidents have marked the date by proclamation: Barack Obama signed one on November 13, 2015 proclaiming November 15, 2015 as America Recycles Day, and the EPA recorded a proclamation from Donald Trump for November 15, 2017. Proclamations in other years could not be confirmed from an acceptable source for this page.

    Timeline

    • 1994 — Texas Recycles Day begins, the idea of two Texas Commission on Environmental Quality employees, Kevin Tuerff and Valerie Davis, who pick November 15 as its date.
    • October 1, 1997 — The National Recycling Coalition and the EPA co-sponsor a press conference at the White House Conference Center announcing a national version, with Vice President Al Gore as honorary chair.
    • November 15, 1997 — The first national America Recycles Day, with at least 40 states participating. The EPA's later anniversary counts imply 1998 instead, and the conflict is unresolved.
    • 2009 — Keep America Beautiful becomes the day's managing organization and promoter.
    • November 13, 2015 — President Barack Obama signs a proclamation designating November 15, 2015 as America Recycles Day.
    • November 15, 2017 — The EPA marks the 20th America Recycles Day and records a proclamation of the date by President Donald Trump.

    How to observe

    Look up your own municipality's accepted-materials list before you put anything in the bin. Recycling rules are set locally, they differ between neighboring towns, and a hopeful guess is how loads get contaminated.

    Read the actual numbers rather than the slogans. The EPA's Facts and Figures series is free and specific: in 2018, the most recent year it covers, the United States generated 292.4 million tons of municipal solid waste and recycled or composted 32.1 percent of it — down from 34.7 percent in 2015. The EPA's own America Recycles Day page frames the same figure against the long view, from under 7 percent in 1960, and points at a national goal of 50 percent by 2030.

    If you want one small thing with a number attached, the EPA notes that recycling ten plastic bottles saves enough energy to power a laptop for more than 25 hours.

    Frequently asked questions

    Is America Recycles Day a federal holiday?

    No. Nothing closes and no one gets the day off. The EPA promotes it and presidents have proclaimed it, but the date carries no legal status.

    What is the US recycling rate?

    The EPA's most recent published figures cover 2018: 292.4 million tons of municipal solid waste generated, of which 32.1 percent was recycled or composted. That is down from 34.7 percent in 2015. The EPA's America Recycles Day page rounds the figure to 32 percent and contrasts it with under 7 percent in 1960.

    Who runs America Recycles Day?

    Keep America Beautiful, which the U.S. Chamber of Commerce Foundation's history dates as the managing organization since 2009. The EPA promotes the day but does not claim to own it.

    Why is it on November 15?

    The date is inherited from Texas Recycles Day, whose two organizers chose it in 1994. No acceptable source explains why they picked that day.

    Is it America Recycles Day or America Recycles Month?

    Both, as of 2026. Keep America Beautiful's own page announces that the day becomes America Recycles Month this November, with a daily challenge, while keeping November 15 as the date itself.
