    National Bundt Day falls on November 15 and honors a cake pan: the fluted, hollow-centered aluminum ring Nordic Ware has made in Minneapolis since 1950. Who created the observance, and when, is not recorded in any source this page would accept, and Nordic Ware promotes the date without claiming to have invented it.

    History

    The origin of this observance is not well documented. Nordic Ware, which owns the Bundt trademark, promotes the date on its own home page — "National Bundt Day is November 15, and we're celebrating all month long!" — but never says who established it, or when. The earliest dated reference this page could find is a University of St. Thomas newsroom post from November 15, 2010, which likewise offers no origin.

    The pan is a different matter. In 1950, members of the Minneapolis chapter of Hadassah asked H. David Dalquist, who ran a small Minneapolis aluminum company, to reproduce a heavy European cake pan. MNopedia, the Minnesota Historical Society's encyclopedia, names Rose Joshua, Fannie Shanfield and Mary Abrahamson, and says the model was a Gugelhupf pan the Abrahamson family had brought from Germany: cast iron, weighing more than fifteen pounds. The Jewish Telegraphic Agency's 2005 obituary of Dalquist gives two of the same names and describes Joshua bringing her heavy iron pan to him. A University of St. Thomas account differs on one detail, calling the pan he was shown ceramic rather than iron. All three agree on the year, and on what he did next: he cast the shape in aluminum, with deeper folds in the flutes.

    The name was his too, and the spelling was deliberate. Per MNopedia, Bundt comes from the German Bund, a gathering or association; Dalquist added the T so the word could be trademarked, and so his pan would not be associated with the German American Bund, the Nazi organization of the 1930s.

    Sales were slow for more than a decade. What changed it was the 1966 Pillsbury Bake-Off, where Ella Rita Helfrich of Houston entered a Tunnel of Fudge cake baked in a Bundt pan and took second place in the contest's seventeenth running. MNopedia records that Pillsbury received more than 200,000 letters asking where the pan could be bought, and the company launched a line of Bundt cake mixes in 1971. One conflict is worth naming: the JTA obituary, relaying Nordic Ware's own account, calls the cake the top-prize winner. MNopedia and St. Thomas both say second place, and this page follows them.

    Timeline

    • 1946 — Nordic Ware is founded in the Minneapolis area with $500 in capital. Its heritage page credits Dotty and Dave Dalquist; the St. Louis Park Historical Society credits Dave and Mark Dalquist with Donald Nygren.
    • 1950 — Members of the Minneapolis chapter of Hadassah ask H. David Dalquist to reproduce a heavy European cake pan, and the first Bundt pan is produced the same year.
    • 1966 — Ella Rita Helfrich's Tunnel of Fudge cake takes second place in the seventeenth Pillsbury Bake-Off, and Pillsbury receives more than 200,000 letters asking where to find the pan.
    • 1971 — Pillsbury launches a line of Bundt cake mixes.
    • January 2, 2005 — H. David Dalquist dies at 86.
    • November 15, 2010 — The earliest dated reference to National Bundt Day this page could locate, in a University of St. Thomas newsroom post.

    How to observe

    Bake something heavy. The shape exists for a reason: the hollow center lets heat reach the middle of a batter too dense to cook through in a loaf tin, which is exactly what the Hadassah bakers wanted in 1950. A pound cake is closer to the original brief than a light sponge.

    Grease the flutes thoroughly; the detail that makes the pan worth owning is the same detail that makes it stick.

    Read the Minnesota Historical Society's MNopedia entry on the Bundt pan. It is free, it is sourced, and it names the three women who asked for the pan in the first place, which most retellings leave out.

    Frequently asked questions

    Who invented the Bundt pan?

    H. David Dalquist of Nordic Ware, in 1950, at the request of members of the Minneapolis chapter of Hadassah who wanted an American version of a heavy European cake pan. Sources differ on whether the pan he was shown was cast iron or ceramic.

    Where does the word Bundt come from?

    From the German Bund, meaning a gathering or association. Per the Minnesota Historical Society, Dalquist added the T so he could trademark the word, and to avoid association with the German American Bund, a Nazi organization of the 1930s.

    Who started National Bundt Day?

    No acceptable source says. Nordic Ware promotes November 15 on its own site but does not claim to have created the day, and no founding organization or first observance could be traced. The origin of this observance is not well documented.

    Is a Bundt cake a particular recipe?

    No. Bundt is a pan shape and a Nordic Ware trademark rather than a recipe, so a Bundt cake is whatever is baked in the pan.

    How many Bundt pans are there?

    The figures disagree and count different things. MNopedia says more than 70 million pans in 100 designs sold worldwide as of 2016; Nordic Ware says the pan is in more than 75 million households; the St. Louis Park Historical Society says over 40 million sold; the 2005 JTA obituary, citing company literature, put more than 45 million in use.
