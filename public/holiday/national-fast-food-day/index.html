    National Fast Food Day falls on November 16, and nobody appears to know who started it. No founder, founding year or sponsoring organization turns up in any government record, news archive or industry body's own material. What can be documented is the thing the day points at: an industry with a traceable beginning in Wichita, Kansas, in 1921 and a measured place in what Americans eat now.

    History

    The origin of this observance is not well documented. The date circulates on holiday-listing sites, none of which say where they got it, and no restaurant association, company or public body claims to have created it. That absence is the honest answer, and it is worth more than a plausible-sounding founder.

    The industry has a documented origin, and the Kansas Historical Society states it plainly: "The fast food industry began in Wichita when J. Walter Anderson developed a new way to cook hamburgers." Anderson and his partner Billy Ingram "opened White Castle in Wichita in 1921," soon added a second location in Kansas City, and expanded into twelve major cities in the eastern United States during the 1920s. What Anderson standardized mattered as much as the cooking: the historical society credits him with "paper hats, paper wrapped hamburgers, a manual for food preparation, and guidelines for employee cleanliness and appearance." The rest of the business copied the template.

    The assembly line came later. Dick and Mac McDonald opened a barbecue restaurant in San Bernardino, California, in 1940. By the company's own account, "In 1948 they took a risk by streamlining their operations and introducing their Speedee Service System featuring 15 cent hamburgers." Ray Kroc visited the brothers in 1954, became their franchise agent, and opened his own McDonald's in Des Plaines, Illinois, on April 15, 1955.

    The phrase arrived last of all. Merriam-Webster dates the first known use of "fast-food" to 1960, roughly four decades after White Castle opened.

    Timeline

    • 1921 — J. Walter Anderson and Billy Ingram open White Castle in Wichita, Kansas. The Kansas Historical Society dates the beginning of the fast food industry to Anderson's method of cooking hamburgers there.
    • 1920s — White Castle adds a second location in Kansas City and expands into twelve major cities in the eastern United States.
    • 1940 — Dick and Mac McDonald open McDonald's Bar-B-Q on Fourteenth and E streets in San Bernardino, California.
    • 1948 — The McDonald brothers introduce their Speedee Service System, built around 15-cent hamburgers.
    • April 15, 1955 — Ray Kroc, the brothers' franchise agent since 1954, opens his own McDonald's in Des Plaines, Illinois.
    • 1960 — The earliest known use in print of the term "fast-food," by Merriam-Webster's dating.

    How to observe

    Read the measurement rather than the marketing. The National Center for Health Statistics tracks fast food intake through the National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey, and its Data Brief No. 533, published in June 2025, reports that during August 2021 to August 2023 "about one-third of adults age 20 and older (32.0%) consumed fast food on a given day." Adults took 11.7% of their calories from fast food over that period, down from 14.1% in 2013 to 2014, and the share falls steadily with age: 15.2% for ages 20 to 39, 11.9% for 40 to 59, and 7.6% for 60 and older.

    Then check your assumptions against the older brief. In 2013 to 2016 the same agency found fast food consumption rising with income rather than falling: 31.7% of adults at or below 130% of the federal poverty level ate fast food on a given day, against 42.0% of adults above 350% of it. It is the opposite of the usual story, and it sits in a federal data brief rather than an opinion column.

    If you want the local version, Wichita is where the Kansas Historical Society says the industry began, and its entry on White Castle is a few hundred words long.

    Frequently asked questions

    Who started National Fast Food Day?

    No one who can be documented. No founder, year or sponsoring organization appears in any government record, news archive or industry body's own material, and the holiday-listing sites that publish the date do not say where they got it.

    Where did fast food begin?

    In Wichita, Kansas, by the Kansas Historical Society's account: J. Walter Anderson developed a new way to cook hamburgers there, and he and Billy Ingram opened White Castle in 1921.

    How old is the term "fast food"?

    Younger than the industry. Merriam-Webster dates its first known use to 1960, about four decades after White Castle opened.

    How many Americans eat fast food on a given day?

    Roughly a third of adults. The National Center for Health Statistics reports 32.0% of adults 20 and older during August 2021 to August 2023, and reported 36.6% of adults for 2013 to 2016, though the two estimates cover different survey periods.

    Do higher-income Americans eat less fast food?

    Not in the 2013 to 2016 data. The National Center for Health Statistics found 31.7% of lower-income adults and 42.0% of higher-income adults ate fast food on a given day.
