    World Mental Health Day is marked on October 10 every year. It was created in 1992 by the World Federation for Mental Health, not by the World Health Organization, though WHO has since become the day's largest amplifier. Its first incarnation was a two-hour television broadcast beamed out of a studio in Tallahassee, Florida, to whichever countries could receive it.

    History

    The World Federation for Mental Health observed the first World Mental Health Day on October 10, 1992. The Federation's own account is specific about who started it: it began as an annual activity of the Federation under its then Deputy Secretary General, Richard Hunter, with aims the Federation describes as "general ones of promoting mental health advocacy and educating the public on relevant issues."

    The format was of its moment. For the day's first three years, one of its central activities was a two-hour telecast broadcast globally through the United States Information Agency's satellite system from studios in Tallahassee, Florida. Federation board members took part from the studio, with live telephone participation from Australia, Chile, England and Zambia and pre-taped segments from Geneva, Atlanta and Mexico City. Reports came back from 27 countries after that first campaign.

    A second strand was political. Rosalynn Carter chaired a First Ladies Committee supporting the day; the First Ladies of 11 countries joined her in the first year, 22 in the second and 31 in the third. That committee still exists, renamed the WFMH International Women Leaders Committee and hosted at the Carter Center in Atlanta.

    The day had no theme at first. In 1994, at the suggestion of then Secretary General Eugene Brody, one was used for the first time: "Improving the Quality of Mental Health Services Throughout the World."

    How to observe

    Read the figures before reading the campaign material. WHO's fact sheet puts the number of people living with a mental disorder at 1.2 billion in 2023, or nearly one in seven people worldwide, with 470 million living with an anxiety disorder and 322 million with depression. The number that explains why the day exists is the next one: only 40 percent of people with psychosis, and about a third of people with depression, receive formal mental health care.

    Find the theme rather than guessing at it. The Federation builds a planning kit around each year's theme and WHO names one for its own campaign; for 2026, WHO lists "Lived experiences heard: real voices, real change." Both are published before October, which is the point of a long lead time the Federation describes as months of preparation rather than a single day's event.

    If you want something concrete rather than symbolic, find out where mental health services in your own area actually are, and what they cost, before anyone needs them. Looking that up on an ordinary Tuesday is a different exercise from looking it up in a crisis.

    Frequently asked questions

    Is World Mental Health Day always October 10?

    Yes. The date is fixed, and the World Federation for Mental Health has commemorated it on October 10 every year since 1992.

    Who created World Mental Health Day?

    The World Federation for Mental Health, as an annual activity started by its then Deputy Secretary General, Richard Hunter. The World Health Organization promotes and amplifies the day but did not create it.

    Does World Mental Health Day have a theme each year?

    Yes, though not at the start. The first theme came in 1994, suggested by then Secretary General Eugene Brody: "Improving the Quality of Mental Health Services Throughout the World." WHO also names a theme for its own campaign each year.

    How many people live with a mental health condition?

    WHO's fact sheet puts it at 1.2 billion people in 2023, nearly one in seven worldwide, with anxiety and depressive disorders the most common. WHO also reports that about a third of people with depression receive formal care.

    Is World Mental Health Day a public holiday?

    No. It is an international awareness day promoted by the World Federation for Mental Health and the World Health Organization, not a legal holiday, and it creates no day off.
