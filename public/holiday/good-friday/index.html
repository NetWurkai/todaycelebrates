    Good Friday is the Friday before Easter, the day Christians commemorate the crucifixion and death of Jesus, and the one day in the year on which the Catholic Church celebrates no Mass. It is not a federal holiday in the United States, although the stock exchanges close for it and eleven states treat it as a holiday of some kind — which makes it one of the harder days on the American calendar to sum up in a sentence.

    History

    Christians kept Friday as a fast from the earliest times, and by the late fourth century Jerusalem had a Good Friday liturgy detailed enough that we can still read a description of it. Egeria, a pilgrim whose account is dated to roughly 381 to 384, records a chair set for the bishop at Golgotha behind the Cross and pilgrims filing past to touch the wood and the titulus with their foreheads and then their eyes. "The emotion shown and the mourning by all the people at every lesson and prayer is wonderful," she wrote. The veneration of the cross began there and entered the Latin liturgy in the seventh or eighth century.

    The date follows Easter, and Easter follows the Council of Nicaea of 325, which acted to put the whole Church on one Sunday. The familiar formula — the first Sunday after the first full moon on or after the vernal equinox — is how the Church later worked the rule out rather than a quotation from Nicaea, and its moon is a tabulated ecclesiastical one, not the astronomical moon.

    The day also has a history worth stating rather than skipping. The Good Friday intercessions long carried a prayer describing the Jews as perfidis, and the kneeling that accompanied every other intercession was omitted for that one. The day was physically dangerous for Jews in medieval Europe, where Jewish houses were commonly attacked with stones. Pope John XXIII dropped perfidis from the Latin prayer in 1959, though the 1962 missal still spoke of the Jews' blindness and their conversion. In 1965 Nostra Aetate held that Christ's passion "cannot be charged against all the Jews, without distinction" nor against the Jews of today, and decried antisemitism "at any time and by anyone." The prayer has been reworded since; it still asks that Jewish hearts be illuminated to recognize Christ, and still troubles many.

    Timeline

    • 325 — The First Council of Nicaea acts to put the whole Church on a single Easter Sunday, fixing Good Friday as the Friday before it.
    • c. 381–384 — Egeria, a pilgrim to Jerusalem, leaves the earliest detailed description of a Good Friday liturgy: the bishop's chair at Golgotha and the veneration of the cross.
    • Good Friday 1727 — Bach's St Matthew Passion is first heard at St Thomas's Church in Leipzig, written for Good Friday vespers.
    • 1959 — Pope John XXIII drops the word perfidis from the Latin Good Friday prayer for the Jewish people.
    • October 28, 1965 — Paul VI promulgates Nostra Aetate, holding that the passion cannot be charged against the Jews as a people and decrying antisemitism by anyone.

    How to observe

    The central service is the Celebration of the Lord's Passion, normally about three in the afternoon and never later than nine at night. It is not a Mass. The celebrant and deacon prostrate themselves in silence to begin; all three readings, including the Passion according to John, are required; the priest is first to venerate the cross; and Communion comes from the sacrament reserved the day before. For Catholics between 18 and 59 this is the second of the year's two obligatory fast days, with abstinence from meat from age 14.

    Beyond the liturgy there is the Three Hour Service between noon and three, a Jesuit-introduced meditation on the seven last words, and the Stations of the Cross, which began with pilgrims tracing the Via Dolorosa in Jerusalem. The day also carries the collection for the Holy Land, established by Paul VI in 1974 to maintain the sacred sites and support local Christian communities.

    Frequently asked questions

    Is Good Friday a federal holiday?

    No. The eleven federal holidays are set by 5 U.S.C. 6103, and Good Friday is not among them. The Federal Reserve does not observe it either, so banks are generally open.

    Then why is the stock market closed?

    The exchanges set their own calendars, and the NYSE and Nasdaq both close. The bond market follows a SIFMA recommendation that varies year to year — a full close in 2025, an early close at noon in 2026 — and even those are advisory only.

    Which states observe Good Friday?

    Eleven, and not in the same way: statutory in Delaware, Indiana, Louisiana, North Carolina and North Dakota; a statutory half day in Kentucky; observed in Hawaii, New Jersey and Tennessee; designated by the governor in Connecticut; optional in Texas, where offices stay open.

    Why is it called "Good" Friday?

    Nobody is certain, and the reference works decline to settle it. The 1913 Catholic Encyclopedia says plainly that "the origin of the term Good is not clear," offering either a corruption of "God's Friday" or "good" in the older sense of holy. Other languages sidestep it: Holy Friday in the Romance languages, Sorrowful Friday in German.

    Do Orthodox Christians keep the same day?

    Usually not, because the Eastern churches reckon Pascha on the Julian calendar. In 2027 the two are five weeks apart — the maximum — with Western Good Friday on March 26 and Orthodox Pascha on May 2. In 2028 they coincide.
