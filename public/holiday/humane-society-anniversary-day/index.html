    Humane Society Anniversary Day, observed on November 22, marks the founding of the organization known for most of its life as the Humane Society of the United States. The date comes from an archive rather than from the organization itself: North Carolina State University Libraries, which holds the group's records, dates the founding to November 22, 1954, while the organization's own history gives only "November 1954." No founding body, proclamation or news report designates the observance; it appears only on calendar sites.

    History

    The chronology in NC State's finding aid for the Humane World for Animals Records is specific: "November 22, 1954 National Humane Society (NHS) founded by a breakaway faction of the American Humane Society (AHA)." The same chronology records that "NHS name changed to The Humane Society of the United States (HSUS)" between December 1956 and January 1957.

    The organization tells it differently. Writing on February 11, 2025, chief executive Kitty Block described the founding this way: "In November 1954, when four animal advocates gathered in a living room in Denver, Colorado, they dared to imagine a world in which such practices would change. With this idea, they founded the Humane Society of the United States." No day, no names, and the later name applied to the earlier organization. The archive and the organization are not reconciled here, and the archive is internally uneven too: its narrative note calls the parent body the American Humane Association while its chronology calls it the American Humane Society, using the same abbreviation for both. Neither source names a founder; NC State's timeline lists Robert Chenoweth as president from 1954 to 1963 and Fred Myers as executive director over the same years, without calling either one.

    The legislative record is firmer. NC State's timeline notes a Humane Slaughter Act passed in 1958 and a Humane Methods of Slaughter Act passed in 1978; the organization says the 1958 law was signed by President Eisenhower less than four years after its founding and calls it "a law our founders championed." That statute now sits at 7 U.S.C. 1901 and following, administered by USDA's Food Safety and Inspection Service, with two sections repealed by Public Law 95-445 on October 10, 1978. The timeline also records a Laboratory Animal Welfare Act in 1966, which USDA's National Agricultural Library states plainly: "The United States signed the Animal Welfare Act (AWA) into law on August 24, 1966." It is enforced by USDA's Animal and Plant Health Inspection Service, and the organization claims its investigations "set the stage" for passage rather than authorship of the bill.

    Timeline

    • November 22, 1954 — The National Humane Society is founded by a breakaway faction of the American Humane Society, per NC State University Libraries' chronology.
    • December 1956 - January 1957 — The National Humane Society renames itself the Humane Society of the United States.
    • 1958 — A Humane Slaughter Act passes; the organization says President Eisenhower signed it and that its founders championed the law.
    • August 24, 1966 — The Animal Welfare Act is signed into law; NC State's timeline records it as the Laboratory Animal Welfare Act.
    • October 10, 1978 — Public Law 95-445 repeals two sections of the slaughter statute; NC State's timeline records a Humane Methods of Slaughter Act the same year.
    • February 2025 — The Humane Society of the United States and Humane Society International adopt the shared name Humane World for Animals.

    How to observe

    Read the finding aid. NC State's collection MC 00674 is public and free, and it is where the November 22 date actually comes from, which is worth seeing for yourself rather than taking secondhand.

    Read the Animal Welfare Act page at USDA's National Agricultural Library, particularly the definition of "animal." The Act covers dogs, cats, nonhuman primates, guinea pigs, hamsters, rabbits and other warm-blooded animals in research, teaching, testing, exhibition and transport. It excludes birds, rats of the genus Rattus and mice of the genus Mus bred for research, and farm animals used for food or fiber. What a famous law leaves out is usually the more useful half.

    Frequently asked questions

    What happened on November 22, 1954?

    According to the chronology in NC State University Libraries' finding aid for the organization's records, the National Humane Society was founded that day by a breakaway faction of the American Humane Society.

    Who founded it?

    No acceptable source names the founders. The organization says four animal advocates met in a living room in Denver, Colorado; NC State's timeline names Robert Chenoweth as president and Fred Myers as executive director from 1954, without describing either as a founder.

    Was it always called the Humane Society of the United States?

    No. The archive records the founding name as the National Humane Society, changed to the Humane Society of the United States between December 1956 and January 1957. Since February 2025 it has operated as Humane World for Animals.

    Is Humane Society Anniversary Day an official observance?

    No. No proclamation, government page, founding body or news report designating it could be found; the date circulates on holiday calendar sites, which is why the founding date here is cited to a university archive instead.

    Which laws is the organization connected to?

    Its own account claims its founders championed the 1958 slaughter law and that its investigations "set the stage" for the 1966 Animal Welfare Act. Both are real federal statutes; the organization's role in them is its own characterization.
