    November 22 is Kimchi Day, and unusually for a food observance, it exists because a national legislature wrote it into a statute. South Korea amended its Kimchi Industry Promotion Act in February 2020 to add an article designating the date, and since 2021 several U.S. state legislatures have adopted the same day by resolution. What none of those documents explains is why November 22 in particular.

    History

    The Kimchi Industry Promotion Act was enacted in South Korea as Act No. 10884 on July 21, 2011. Kimchi Day arrived nine years later, as Article 20-2, inserted by Act No. 16963 and marked in the statute "[This Article Newly Inserted on Feb. 11, 2020]." Its first paragraph provides that "November 22 of every year shall be designated as the Kimchi Day to promote the kimchi industry, to inherit and develop kimchi-related culture, and to inform the people of the nutritional value and importance of kimchi." A second paragraph lets national and local governments hold events or run education and publicity toward that purpose. The same act provides for a World Institute of Kimchi.

    Korea's Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism, writing in November 2022, confirms the 2020 designation and notes that California, Virginia, New York and Michigan mark the day as well. California moved first among them. House Resolution 52, introduced on May 25, 2021 by Assembly Members Choi and Quirk-Silva and adopted on August 23, 2021, resolves that "November 22nd of each year shall be celebrated as Kimchi Day." Virginia followed with House Joint Resolution 147, agreed to by the House of Delegates on February 9, 2022 and by the Senate on March 1, 2022, designating November 22 "in 2022 and in each succeeding year."

    Why that date is the part no official source answers. The explanation in general circulation — eleven ingredients, twenty-two health benefits — appears in The Hill's reporting on the first Maryland and Virginia observances, which ties the day to "the 22 health benefits of the food and the 11 different ingredients used to make the side dish." That is a news outlet's account, not the statute's, and the Korean government pages found here offer no reason at all.

    Timeline

    • 37 B.C. - 7 A.D. — California's resolution dates kimchi's origin to "the early period of the Three Kingdoms of Korea"; Virginia's puts it between the 1st century B.C. and the 7th century A.D. Both are legislative findings rather than archaeology.
    • July 21, 2011 — South Korea enacts the Kimchi Industry Promotion Act, Act No. 10884.
    • 2013 — UNESCO inscribes kimjang on the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity, at the committee's eighth session.
    • February 11, 2020 — Act No. 16963 inserts Article 20-2, designating November 22 as Kimchi Day.
    • August 23, 2021 — California adopts House Resolution 52.
    • February 9 and March 1, 2022 — Virginia's House and Senate agree to House Joint Resolution 147.

    How to observe

    Read the law. The Kimchi Industry Promotion Act is short in English translation and freely available, which is more than most food holidays can offer: you can check the designation against the text rather than take a calendar site's word for it. California's HR 52 and Virginia's HJ 147 are shorter still, and both are worth reading for the spectacle of a legislature building a factual record about a side dish.

    Make a batch instead of buying one. UNESCO's entry for kimjang — the collective making and sharing of kimchi, inscribed in 2013 — says that "Late autumn is Kimjang season," which puts November 22 inside the working season rather than next to it. The same entry describes a year-long run-up: seafood secured in spring, sea salt bought in summer, red chili peppers dried and ground in late summer.

    Frequently asked questions

    Is Kimchi Day a public holiday in South Korea?

    No. Article 20-2 designates the date and allows governments to hold events and run publicity, but it is a statutory observance, not a day off.

    Why is Kimchi Day on November 22?

    No Korean government source found here gives a reason. The account in circulation — eleven ingredients, twenty-two benefits — is reported by The Hill in its coverage of the first Maryland and Virginia observances, and is repeated here as that outlet's explanation rather than the law's.

    Which U.S. states recognize it?

    California adopted a resolution in 2021 and Virginia in 2022. Korea's culture ministry also names New York and Michigan, and The Hill reports a Maryland proclamation under then-Governor Larry Hogan. No federal designation appears in any source acceptable here.

    Is kimchi the same thing as kimjang?

    No. Kimjang is the practice of making and sharing kimchi in quantity, generally in late autumn. What UNESCO inscribed in 2013 was that practice, not the food.

    Did UNESCO declare kimchi a world heritage food?

    Not quite, and Virginia's own resolution gets this wrong: it calls the 2013 action a "National Intangible Cultural Heritage Item," while UNESCO's record lists kimjang on the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.
