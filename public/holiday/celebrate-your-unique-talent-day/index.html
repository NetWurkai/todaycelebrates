    Celebrate Your Unique Talent Day is observed on November 24, and nothing is known about where it came from: no founder, no first year, no reason for the date. What can be said with a source is odder — that the English word "talent" began as a unit of weight, took its modern meaning from a parable about money, and that the federal government counts how many American adults actually make or perform anything.

    History

    The origin of this observance is not well documented. It appears only on calendar aggregators, which are not acceptable sources, and no organization, school system, arts body or news outlet consulted for this page claims it or explains November 24.

    The word has a better-documented past. Merriam-Webster traces "talent" through Middle English and Old English talente to the Latin talentum, a unit of weight or money, and before that to the Greek talanton, meaning the pan of a scale and then a weight. The first known use in English, before the twelfth century, carried exactly that sense: an ancient unit of weight. A talent was a large amount — which is the point of the parable the dictionary credits for everything that came after.

    Merriam-Webster attributes the modern senses of the word, from "the natural endowments of a person" to a person of ability, to the parable of the talents in Matthew 25:14-30. In the King James Version a householder going abroad gives his servants money to manage: "And unto one he gave five talents, to another two, and to another one, to every man according to his several ability." The servants who put the money to work are commended; the one who buries his share is not. Because the sums were entrusted to individuals, the English word slid from the coin to the capacity, and a buried talent became a figure of speech for an unused gift.

    How many people exercise one is, unusually for a day like this, a matter of federal record. The National Endowment for the Arts runs the Survey of Public Participation in the Arts with the Census Bureau; its 2022 round surveyed 40,718 adults, and its highlights report, published in October 2023, found that "more than half (52 percent) of all adults created and/or performed art in the 2022 survey period" — about 129 million people. The most common activity is not what the phrase "unique talent" usually conjures. Social dancing, including dancing at weddings and clubs, came first at 21.8 percent of adults, with singing behind it at 20.2 percent, down about five percentage points from 2017. Then photography as an artistic activity at 13.3 percent, weaving, knitting or sewing 11.7, playing an instrument 11.4, leatherwork, metalwork or woodwork 9.0, creative writing 6.9, pottery, ceramics or jewelry 4.4, and acting 1.6.

    Timeline

    • Before the twelfth century — The first known use of "talent" in English, meaning an ancient unit of weight, from Latin talentum and Greek talanton.
    • Middle English onward — The senses meaning natural ability, and a person of ability, enter English from the parable of the talents in Matthew 25:14-30.
    • 2017 — In the previous Survey of Public Participation in the Arts, 25.4 percent of adults reported singing and 13.8 percent artistic photography.
    • July 2022 — The NEA's survey of 40,718 adults finds 52 percent created or performed art in the preceding twelve months; social dancing leads at 21.8 percent.
    • October 2023 — The NEA publishes those figures in "Arts Participation Patterns in 2022."

    How to observe

    Pick the talent you have stopped practicing rather than the one you are good at. The NEA's figures show where the teaching happens: 29.1 percent of adults learned something about an art form in the survey period, but only 11.8 percent through a class or lesson, and 26 percent from friends, family or self-teaching. It is mostly informal, which means the most useful thing to do with a day like this is show one other person how to do the thing you can do.

    If you want the parable's version of the assignment, it is not about performance. The servant who is criticized in Matthew 25 has not lost anything; he has kept his talent safe and buried. Taking it out of the ground is the whole instruction.

    Frequently asked questions

    Who created Celebrate Your Unique Talent Day?

    No source consulted says. The day appears on calendar aggregators with no founder, no first year and no explanation of the date, and those sites are not acceptable sources.

    Why does "talent" mean ability when it used to mean money?

    Because of the parable of the talents in Matthew 25:14-30, which Merriam-Webster credits for the modern senses of the word. A talent was a unit of weight and then of money; in the parable it is entrusted to individuals according to their ability, and English moved the word from the sum to the capacity.

    How many American adults actually make or perform art?

    About 52 percent, or roughly 129 million, in the twelve months ending July 2022, according to the National Endowment for the Arts. Social dancing was the most common activity at 21.8 percent of adults, followed by singing at 20.2 percent.

    Is this the same as National Talent Day or a talent show day?

    Not that any source establishes. No sponsoring organization stands behind this date to say what it covers, which is why this page sticks to what the word and the survey data support.
