    Veterans Day

    Send viaEmailTextFacebookXWhatsApp
    Date
    Wednesday, November 11, 2026
    Recurs
    Every year on November 11
    Category
    Celebration

    Veterans Day falls on November 11 and honors everyone who served honorably in the United States armed forces, in wartime or peacetime, living or dead. The date is not symbolic: it is the hour the guns stopped in the First World War, 11 a.m. on November 11, 1918. For its first 36 years the holiday was called Armistice Day, and for seven years in the 1970s it was not observed on November 11 at all.

    History

    The armistice between the Allies and Germany took effect at the eleventh hour of November 11, 1918, signed at Compiegne in France. It was a ceasefire, not a peace — the Treaty of Versailles came the following year — but it is the moment the fighting ended, and it is why this holiday has an hour as well as a date.

    President Wilson proclaimed the first Armistice Day in November 1919, speaking of "solemn pride in the heroism of those who died in the country's service." The original form of the observance was two minutes of suspended business beginning at 11 a.m., followed by parades and public meetings. On October 20, 1921, Congress declared November 11 of that year a legal federal holiday; that was the day the Unknown Soldier was buried at Arlington. In 1926 Congress directed the President to issue an Armistice Day proclamation every year, and on May 13, 1938, it made November 11 a permanent legal federal holiday.

    All of that was about one war. The change came from a veteran of the next one. In 1947 Raymond Weeks, a World War II veteran, organized a National Veterans Day observance in Birmingham, Alabama, on November 11 — the date was still officially Armistice Day — and pressed for a holiday that covered everyone who had served. Representative Edward Rees of Kansas carried the bill, and President Eisenhower signed it on June 1, 1954. President Reagan presented Weeks with the Presidential Citizens Medal in November 1982.

    Congress then moved the holiday and had to move it back. The Monday Holiday Law of 1968 set the federal observance on the fourth Monday in October, effective 1971. It did not hold: states returned to November 11 through the early 1970s, most of them by 1975, and legislation that year restored the federal date to November 11, effective 1978.

    Timeline

    • November 11, 1918 — An armistice between the Allies and Germany takes effect at the eleventh hour, signed at Compiegne, France, ending the fighting of the First World War.

    How to observe

    Keep the two minutes. The first Armistice Day was built around a two-minute pause at 11 a.m., and it remains the most exact way to mark a holiday that commemorates an hour rather than a day.

    Record a veteran. The Library of Congress runs the Veterans History Project, which collects first-hand accounts from American war veterans and accepts interviews submitted by the public; it publishes a field kit with the forms, the release and interview guidance. An afternoon with a relative and a recorder puts their account into the national collection permanently.

    Get the courtesies right. This day honors all who served; the day for those who died in service is Memorial Day, and the poppy — from John McCrae's 1915 poem "In Flanders Fields" — is traditionally worn then rather than now. There is also no apostrophe in Veterans Day: the Department of Veterans Affairs is explicit that the day does not belong to veterans, it honors them.

    Frequently asked questions

    Is Veterans Day a federal holiday?

    Yes. It is one of the eleven legal public holidays listed in 5 U.S.C. 6103, and it is observed on November 11 whatever the weekday. When November 11 falls on a Saturday, most federal employees observe it the preceding Friday; when it falls on a Sunday, the following Monday.

    What is the difference between Veterans Day and Memorial Day?

    Memorial Day honors military personnel who died in service, particularly those killed in battle or from wounds sustained in battle. Veterans Day honors everyone who served honorably, in wartime or peacetime, and is largely a day for thanking living veterans.

    Is there an apostrophe in Veterans Day?

    No. The Department of Veterans Affairs is explicit about it: the day does not belong to veterans, so the s is not possessive.

    Why November 11, and why 11 a.m.?

    The armistice that ended the fighting of the First World War took effect at the eleventh hour of November 11, 1918. The first observances in 1919 were built around a two-minute pause at that hour.

    Was Veterans Day ever held in October?

    Yes, from 1971 through 1977. The Monday Holiday Law of 1968 moved the federal observance to the fourth Monday in October. States went back to November 11 anyway, most by 1975, and 1975 legislation returned the federal date to November 11 effective 1978.
