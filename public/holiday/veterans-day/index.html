    Veterans Day

    Send viaEmailTextFacebookXWhatsApp
    Date
    Wednesday, November 11, 2026
    Recurs
    Every year on November 11
    Category
    Celebration

    Veterans Day falls on November 11 and honors everyone who has served in the United States armed forces, living or dead. The date is not symbolic: it is the hour the First World War stopped. For seven years the federal observance was moved to a Monday in October, which went badly enough that Congress reversed it.

    History

    The fighting in the First World War stopped under an armistice that took effect, in the phrase the Department of Veterans Affairs still uses, at "the eleventh hour of the eleventh day of the eleventh month" of 1918. The Treaty of Versailles was not signed until June 28, 1919, so November 11 marks the ceasefire rather than the peace.

    President Woodrow Wilson proclaimed the first Armistice Day commemoration in November 1919, saying the day would be "filled with solemn pride in the heroism of those who died in the country's service." Congress passed a concurrent resolution on June 4, 1926, and an act of May 13, 1938 made November 11 a legal holiday dedicated to world peace, honoring the veterans of that one war.

    Two more wars made that too narrow. Public Law 380, passed by the 83rd Congress on June 1, 1954, struck the word "Armistice" from the 1938 act and inserted "Veterans," widening the day to everyone who had served; President Eisenhower issued the first Veterans Day proclamation on October 8 that year.

    Then the Uniform Holiday Bill, Public Law 90-363, signed June 28, 1968, moved the observance to a Monday, and the first Veterans Day under it fell on October 25, 1971. Moving a day named for an hour proved unpopular, and on September 20, 1975 President Ford signed Public Law 94-97, returning it to November 11 beginning in 1978.

    Timeline

    • 1918 — November 11: fighting in the First World War ends under an armistice taking effect at the eleventh hour of the eleventh day of the eleventh month.
    • 1919 — November: President Wilson proclaims the first Armistice Day commemoration.
    • 1926 — June 4: Congress passes a concurrent resolution marking the end of the war.
    • 1938 — May 13: an act of Congress makes November 11 a legal holiday, dedicated to world peace and called Armistice Day.
    • 1954 — June 1: Public Law 380 replaces "Armistice" with "Veterans"; Eisenhower issues the first Veterans Day proclamation on October 8.
    • 1975 — September 20: President Ford signs Public Law 94-97, undoing the 1968 move to Mondays and returning the observance to November 11 from 1978.

    How to observe

    The national ceremony is at Arlington National Cemetery, and it starts on the hour. The VA says it commences precisely at 11:00 a.m. with a wreath laying at the Tomb of the Unknowns, then moves into the Memorial Amphitheater for a parade of colors by veterans' organizations and remarks from dignitaries.

    Arlington is not the only official observance, which is the part most people miss. The Veterans Day National Committee — almost fifty veteran-serving organizations — designates regional sites around the country to serve as models for other communities planning their own, and publishes them. Finding the designated site near you is more useful than assuming the ceremony only happens in Virginia.

    If you write anything public about the day, get the spelling right. There is no apostrophe, and the National Archives has a short, unusually direct page explaining why.

    Frequently asked questions

    Is Veterans Day a federal holiday?

    Yes. It is one of the eleven holidays set by 5 U.S.C. 6103. Under that statute, when November 11 falls on a Saturday most federal employees are given the preceding Friday; when it falls on a Sunday, the following Monday.

    What is the difference between Veterans Day and Memorial Day?

    Veterans Day honors everyone who served, living or dead — the VA describes the national ceremony as intended to honor and thank all who served in the United States Armed Forces. Memorial Day began as Decoration Day, for decorating the graves of those who died in service, and that is still its subject.

    Why is there no apostrophe in Veterans Day?

    Because the day does not belong to one veteran. The National Archives puts it plainly: that is Veterans without any apostrophe before the s or anywhere else. The word is working as an adjective.

    Why was Veterans Day once observed in October?

    The Uniform Holiday Bill, Public Law 90-363, moved it to a Monday, and the first Veterans Day under that law was October 25, 1971. President Ford signed Public Law 94-97 on September 20, 1975, returning it to November 11 from 1978 on.

    What happens at 11 a.m. on November 11?

    At Arlington National Cemetery, a wreath laying at the Tomb of the Unknowns, timed to the hour the 1918 armistice took effect, followed by a ceremony in the Memorial Amphitheater.
