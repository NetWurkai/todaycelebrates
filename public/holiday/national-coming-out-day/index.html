    National Coming Out Day is observed on October 11, and has been since 1988. The date is not arbitrary: it is the anniversary of the Second National March on Washington for Lesbian and Gay Rights, held October 11, 1987, the weekend the AIDS Memorial Quilt was unfolded in full for the first time. The day was built on a single premise one of its founders kept repeating, that most people who believe they know no gay person are wrong.

    History

    The march came first. On October 11, 1987, the Second National March on Washington for Lesbian and Gay Rights filled the capital with a crowd the University of Iowa Libraries puts at around 750,000. Marchers called for legal recognition of lesbian and gay relationships, repeal of laws criminalizing sodomy between consenting adults, and legal protection against discrimination. It was also the first time ACT UP drew national media coverage.

    That same weekend, the NAMES Project AIDS Memorial Quilt was displayed in full for the first time, on the National Mall. It held 1,920 panels and covered a space larger than a football field. Six teams of eight volunteers unfolded the sections at sunrise while the 1,920 names were read aloud, and half a million people came to see it over the weekend. The response sent the Quilt on a four-month, 20-city national tour the following spring and summer.

    A year after the march, on October 11, 1988, Jean O'Leary and the psychologist Robert Eichberg organized the first National Coming Out Day through an organization of the same name. Eichberg had been running a workshop called The Experience since 1978 to encourage people to come out to family and friends; he died of complications of AIDS in 1995, at 50. In 1993 the National Coming Out Day organization merged with what was then the Human Rights Campaign Fund, which created its National Coming Out Project in 1996 and runs the day still.

    Timeline

    • 1978 — Robert Eichberg founds The Experience, a community-based workshop encouraging people to come out to family and friends.
    • October 11, 1987 — The Second National March on Washington for Lesbian and Gay Rights draws a turnout the University of Iowa Libraries puts at around 750,000. The NAMES Project AIDS Memorial Quilt is displayed in full for the first time, 1,920 panels on the National Mall.
    • October 11, 1988 — Jean O'Leary and Robert Eichberg organize the first National Coming Out Day, on the march's first anniversary.
    • 1988 — The Quilt's reception at the march sends it on a four-month, 20-city national tour the following spring and summer.
    • 1993 — The National Coming Out Day organization merges with the Human Rights Campaign Fund, now the Human Rights Campaign.
    • 1996 — The Human Rights Campaign creates the National Coming Out Project, extending the work past the one day.

    How to observe

    The founders' case for a fixed date was an argument about proximity rather than ceremony. "Most people think they don't know anyone gay or lesbian, and in fact everybody does," Eichberg said in 1993. A designated day was a way to make that arithmetic visible all at once, in a year when very little else would.

    What the day asks of you therefore depends on which side of it you are on. If you are considering coming out, the date is a prompt and not a deadline, and it is worth saying plainly that the calculation is not the same for everyone: housing, employment, family support and local law all vary, and a decision deferred is not a decision failed. The Human Rights Campaign, which has organized the day since 1993, publishes guides written for that calculation specifically.

    If you are not coming out, the task is smaller and duller. Most of what makes a disclosure safe happens in the months before it, in how the people nearby talk when they assume no one is listening.

    Frequently asked questions

    Is National Coming Out Day always October 11?

    Yes. The date marks the anniversary of the Second National March on Washington for Lesbian and Gay Rights, held October 11, 1987.

    Who founded National Coming Out Day?

    Jean O'Leary and Dr. Robert Eichberg, a psychologist, who organized the first one on October 11, 1988 through the National Coming Out Day organization. That organization merged with the Human Rights Campaign Fund in 1993.

    Why is National Coming Out Day on October 11?

    It is the first anniversary of the 1987 Second National March on Washington for Lesbian and Gay Rights, the march at which the AIDS Memorial Quilt was displayed in full for the first time.

    Is National Coming Out Day a federal holiday?

    No. It has no official status, creates no day off, and is organized by a private advocacy organization rather than by any government.

    What happened to the Quilt displayed at the 1987 march?

    The response to that first display sent it on a four-month, 20-city national tour in the spring and summer of 1988. The Quilt is now held by the National AIDS Memorial.
