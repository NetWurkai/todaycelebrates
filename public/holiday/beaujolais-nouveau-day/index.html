    Beaujolais Nouveau Day is the single date on which the year's Beaujolais Nouveau may go on sale — a young red wine bottled weeks after the harvest, released on the third Thursday of November, which in 2026 is November 19. The date is a rule rather than a marketing choice: Inter Beaujolais, the region's trade body, traces it to a French decree of 1985. In 2022, 16.5 million bottles of Beaujolais and Beaujolais Villages Nouveaux were sold, 6.5 million of them exported to more than 100 countries.

    History

    The appellation came first. AOC Beaujolais was created in 1937, and at that point the region's wines could not be released until after December 15 of the harvest year. A wine sold within weeks of picking had no legal route to market.

    That changed around 1951. Inter Beaujolais's own timeline marks 15 November 1951 as the point when "Beaujolais Nouveau wines were first commercialised"; Decanter describes the same year as a regulatory change by which "the official release date became 15 November." Neither names a decree or an issuing body, so only the year and the effect are established here.

    The modern date arrived in 1985, when, in Inter Beaujolais's words, "a decree set the third Thursday of November as their release date." The sources diverge on who did it: the trade body says only "a French decree," while Decanter attributes the decision to the INAO, France's appellation authority. Neither gives the decree a number or a date, so the discrepancy is recorded rather than resolved. What both agree on is the rule, and that it applies only to the Beaujolais and Beaujolais Villages appellations — the ten crus do not make Nouveau.

    The celebration is older than the regulation and partly borrowed. Inter Beaujolais links the third-Thursday ritual to the feast of Saint Martin, and credits the 1950s with taking the arrival beyond the region, with the merchant Georges Duboeuf making it an annual event. Japan, now the largest export market, came on from the 1980s. The slogan that travels with it — "Le Beaujolais Nouveau est arrivé!" — has, as Decanter puts it, been adopted worldwide.

    In the glass it is gamay: specifically gamay noir à jus blanc, which Inter Beaujolais says covers 96% of the area producing Beaujolais wines. The grapes are mostly hand-harvested and vinified in whole bunches to keep the aromas and the fruit, by the semi-carbonic maceration traditional in Beaujolais — which Decanter describes for nouveau simply as carbonic maceration. The speed is the point: fermentation inside intact berries gives the soft, high-toned, low-tannin wine that can be drunk weeks after picking.

    One number is worth flagging. Inter Beaujolais's milestones page describes "500,000 hectolitres of Beaujolais Nouveau produced each year," while its appellation page says "over 160,000 hectolitres are drunk" annually — and its 2023 press kit puts the whole Beaujolais AOC, every color, at close to 510,000 hectolitres in 2022. The 500,000 looks like the appellation total, not the Nouveau total, so this page uses the press kit's bottle counts, which carry a stated vintage year.

    Timeline

    • 1937 — AOC Beaujolais is created; the region's wines cannot then be released until after December 15 of the harvest year.
    • 15 November 1951 — Inter Beaujolais dates the first commercialization of Beaujolais Nouveau to this point; Decanter describes the same year as the one in which the official release date became November 15.
    • 1950s — The arrival spreads beyond the region, with the merchant Georges Duboeuf making it an annual event.
    • 1980s — Japan becomes an export market; it is now the largest, at 2.3 million bottles in 2022.
    • 1985 — A French decree fixes the third Thursday of November as the release date for Beaujolais and Beaujolais Villages Nouveaux.
    • 2022 — 16.5 million bottles are sold, about a quarter of all Beaujolais commercialized, with 6.5 million exported to more than 100 countries.

    How to observe

    Buy a bottle on the day and drink it that week. This is the rare wine where youth is the whole proposition.

    Taste it beside something from the same region that was not made this way — a Beaujolais Villages, or one of the crus. Side by side, semi-carbonic maceration stops being a term and becomes a flavor you can point at.

    In the United States the pairing is seasonal rather than traditional: the release lands in Thanksgiving week, and Decanter notes that Beaujolais Nouveau has become synonymous with Thanksgiving meals there. In the region itself the night is a festival — Beaujeu's Sarmentelles runs to a barrel being tapped and fireworks at midnight.

    Frequently asked questions

    When is Beaujolais Nouveau released?

    On the third Thursday of November, which in 2026 falls on November 19. Inter Beaujolais traces the rule to a French decree of 1985; before that the date was November 15.

    Who sets the date?

    Accounts differ on the authority. Inter Beaujolais says only that "a decree" fixed it in 1985, while Decanter credits the INAO, France's appellation institute. Neither gives the decree a number or an exact date, so both versions are reported rather than merged.

    What grape is it made from?

    Gamay — gamay noir à jus blanc, which Inter Beaujolais says accounts for 96% of the area producing Beaujolais wines. Only the Beaujolais and Beaujolais Villages appellations may produce Nouveau.

    Why can it be sold so soon after the harvest?

    Because of how it is made: mostly hand-picked and fermented in whole bunches, by the semi-carbonic maceration traditional in Beaujolais, which gives a soft, aromatic, low-tannin wine within weeks rather than months.

    How much is made, and who drinks it?

    In 2022, 16.5 million bottles were commercialized, about 25% of all Beaujolais sold. More than 10.1 million bottles went to the French market and 6.5 million were exported to more than 100 countries, led by Japan at 2.3 million, the United States at 1.2 million and the United Kingdom at 707,000.
