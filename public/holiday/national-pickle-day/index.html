    National Pickle Day is marked on November 14 and has no documented origin. The pickled-vegetable industry's own trade association, in business since 1893, did create a national pickle observance — but it is a week in May, and the association's site does not mention November 14 at all. What can be documented is the industry, which is considerably older than the day attached to it.

    History

    The origin of this observance is not well documented. Pickle Packers International, the trade association for the pickled-vegetable industry and the most obvious candidate for having invented a pickle holiday, makes no claim to it: neither its history page nor its home page mentions National Pickle Day or November 14.

    What the association does document is a different observance. Its history dates the founding of the National Pickle Packers Association to 1893, as a promotional agency responsible for educating consumers about the benefits of pickles. The association was incorporated in the State of Illinois on December 14, 1933, and changed its name to Pickle Packers International, Inc. on December 9, 1963. The first National Pickle Week ran May 20 to 28, 1949. It now runs ten days as International Pickle Week, built around Memorial Day weekend and the start of cookout season.

    So the sourced American pickle observance is a week in late May, promoted by the industry since 1949. Whoever moved it to a single day in November left no record that this run could find.

    The crop behind it is easier to count. USDA's Vegetables 2023 Summary puts United States cucumber acreage at 81,900 harvested acres in 2023, down from 84,500 in 2022 and 88,100 in 2021, producing 14,256,000 hundredweight with a utilized value of about $290 million.

    How to observe

    Make a batch, and notice which kind you are making. The National Center for Home Food Preservation separates fermented pickles, where salt favors the growth of desirable bacteria while inhibiting the growth of others and regular dills cure for about three weeks, from fresh-pack or quick-process pickles, which are not fermented at all: they are brined for a few hours, drained, and covered with hot vinegar and seasonings. Most jars sold as pickles are the second kind, and the difference is a matter of days versus weeks.

    If you do make them, follow a tested recipe exactly. The Center is blunt about why. The level of acidity in a pickled product is as important to its safety as it is to taste and texture, because there must be a minimum, uniform level of acid throughout the mixed product to prevent the growth of botulinum bacteria. It recommends white distilled or cider vinegar at 5 percent acidity, and says plainly: do not alter vinegar, food, or water proportions in a recipe or use a vinegar with unknown acidity.

    Frequently asked questions

    Who created National Pickle Day?

    No one this run could document. Pickle Packers International, the industry's trade association since 1893, does not mention the day or the November 14 date anywhere on its history or home pages, and no founding organization, government page or news report establishing the day could be found.

    Is National Pickle Day the same as National Pickle Week?

    No, and the week is the one with a paper trail. Pickle Packers International held the first National Pickle Week from May 20 to 28, 1949, and runs it today as a ten-day International Pickle Week around Memorial Day weekend.

    Are pickles fermented?

    Some are. The National Center for Home Food Preservation distinguishes fermented pickles, which cure in salt brine for about three weeks, from fresh-pack or quick-process pickles, which are not fermented and are simply brined briefly and covered with hot vinegar.

    How many pickles do Americans eat?

    The trade association's site says 20 billion a year, with no source and no year attached to the figure, so it is repeated here as the association's claim rather than as a measurement. USDA's own counted figure is the crop: 81,900 acres of cucumbers harvested in 2023, worth about $290 million.

    Is November 14 a day off?

    No. National Pickle Day has no official status and nothing closes for it.
