    Mickey Mouse Day falls on November 18, the date Disney itself treats as Mickey's birthday. It marks the 1928 opening of Steamboat Willie at the Colony Theater in New York, the short that put the character in front of a paying audience with synchronized sound after two earlier Mickey cartoons had failed to find a distributor. The date is younger than the mouse: for four decades the studio moved the birthday around the calendar before an archivist settled it.

    History

    No founding organization, proclamation or government designation for a "Mickey Mouse Day" could be found; the pages carrying the name are daily-holiday aggregators, which this site does not cite. The date itself is well documented, and not arbitrary.

    On November 18, 1928, Steamboat Willie opened at the Colony Theater in New York. D23, Disney's own fan organization, writes that "the first Mickey Mouse cartoon short was released at the Colony Theatre in New York" that day, and calls it the first synchronized sound cartoon, loosely parodying Steamboat Bill, Jr. The Museum of Modern Art, which holds the film, says it "was given an initial modest run" there and credits it as designed and animated by Ub Iwerks with direction from Walt Disney. Two Mickey shorts came before it and went nowhere: Plane Crazy and The Gallopin' Gaucho were silent and, as the Walt Disney Family Museum puts it, had not "succeeded in finding a distributor."

    The birthday then moved for forty years. "November 18 has become the official day of observation of Mickey's 'birthday,' but it was not always so," Jim Korkis wrote for that museum in 2011. Walt Disney had given a different date in Film Pictorial on September 30, 1933: Mickey "was born on October 1, 1928. That was the date on which his first picture started." Until the 1970s, Korkis writes, the date landed "anywhere between September and December, whenever there was a new Disney cartoon being released." With the fiftieth approaching, Disney archivist Dave Smith settled it by "checking through correspondence, reviews from New York newspapers, and finally finding a program from the Colony Theater." No source found gives the year he decided it. The same date is also Minnie's birthday.

    Two later dates are matters of federal record. The Librarian of Congress named Steamboat Willie to the National Film Registry in 1998, in a class of 25 announced that November. In 2024 the film's copyright ran out: the Copyright Office states that "the entire film-including the animation portraying the characters, soundtrack, and other elements" is no longer protected, while noting that this Mickey "appears without some of the features audiences know him for today, including his red shorts and white gloves."

    Timeline

    • November 18, 1928 - Steamboat Willie opens at the Colony Theater in New York, the first Mickey Mouse short released with synchronized sound.
    • September 30, 1933 - Walt Disney tells Film Pictorial that Mickey "was born on October 1, 1928," the day his first picture started.
    • September 28, 1935 - The seventh birthday is celebrated and promoted as "Mickey Mouse's Lucky Seventh Birthday."
    • October 28, 1968 - The fortieth birthday is "officially celebrated," still not in November.
    • 1998 - Steamboat Willie is named to the National Film Registry, in a class of 25 announced November 16.
    • 2024 - The 1928 film enters the United States public domain; later versions of the character are not covered.

    How to observe

    Watch the film instead of reading about it. Steamboat Willie has been in the United States public domain since the start of 2024, so complete copies now circulate freely and legally.

    Then watch Plane Crazy, the Mickey short that came first and failed to sell. Seeing the silent version beside the sound version explains why November 18, 1928 is the date anyone remembers.

    In New York, the Colony still stands as the Broadway Theatre; the Walt Disney Family Museum records that a plaque naming the site as Mickey's official birthplace was installed there for the fiftieth birthday.

    Frequently asked questions

    Is Mickey Mouse Day always November 18?

    Yes. The date is fixed: it comes from the November 18, 1928 opening of Steamboat Willie at New York's Colony Theater. In 2026 it falls on a Wednesday.

    Is November 18 officially Mickey Mouse's birthday?

    It is the date Disney uses. The Walt Disney Family Museum credits archivist Dave Smith with settling on the Steamboat Willie premiere, after working through correspondence, newspaper reviews and a Colony Theater program. No source found names the year.

    Was Steamboat Willie the first Mickey Mouse cartoon?

    It was the first released, not the first made. Plane Crazy and The Gallopin' Gaucho were finished earlier as silents and had not found a distributor. D23 calls Steamboat Willie the first Mickey short released; the Walt Disney Family Museum calls Plane Crazy the first Mickey short.

    Did Walt Disney give a different birthday?

    Yes. In Film Pictorial on September 30, 1933 he said Mickey "was born on October 1, 1928," the day his first picture started production. Before the 1970s the birthday moved between September and December.

    Is Mickey Mouse in the public domain?

    The 1928 Steamboat Willie film is, in the United States, as of 2024. The Copyright Office notes that the whole film is no longer protected and that copyright never covered the names themselves. That version of the character lacks features added later, such as the red shorts and white gloves.
